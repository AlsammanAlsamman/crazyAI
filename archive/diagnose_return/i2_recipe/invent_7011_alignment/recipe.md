CHOSEN SEED: I fold the lattice-sheet along its slanting middle so cells of equal row-and-column sum press flush into a single crease.

1. Take the two boughs of symbols. Hang the first from a row of lamp-posts running downward, one symbol to a post; hang the second from a row running across, one symbol to a post. Where a down-post and an across-post cross, an unlit cell hangs. Leave every cell dark for now — the sheet is nothing but empty windows.

2. Number each cell by adding its down-post's place to its across-post's place. All cells sharing one sum lie along a single slant. That slant is a crease, and the creases are what you will work with; you will never touch a cell except through the crease it belongs to.

3. Light the first crease by hand — the one whose sum is nothing at all, the single corner cell where the topmost down-post crosses the leftmost across-post. Set its spark to no-cost: the two boughs have not yet disagreed about anything. Hold this crease in your left hand.

4. Light the second crease by hand as well — its two cells are a bare slip-in and a bare slip-out, so set each spark to the cost of one slip. Hold this crease in your right hand. You now hold two creases and nothing else.

5. Now fold forward. To make the next crease, walk along its slant cell by cell. For each cell, look at the symbol on its down-post and the symbol on its across-post, and let those two symbols choose the cell's color: if they are the same symbol, the crossing costs nothing; if they differ, the crossing costs one disagreement.

6. For that same cell, read through the fold at the three layers pressed beneath it. Beneath, in the crease in your left hand, lies the spark for agreeing straight through — add the crossing cost from step 5 to it. Beside it, in the crease in your right hand, lie two sparks: one for slipping the down-bough out of step, one for slipping the across-bough out of step — add the cost of one slip to each of these.

7. Take whichever of those three sums is least. That is the new spark. Set it on the cell and move to the next cell along the slant. Where a slant runs off the edge of the lattice — no post to cross there — treat the missing layer as unreachable and simply do not offer it as a choice.

8. When the whole slant is lit, you hold three creases: the one in your left hand, the one in your right, and the new one. Let the left hand go slack. Drift that crease off into the gradient; it has given its answer and there is no ground to store it on. Pass the right-hand crease into the left, and take the new crease into the right.

9. Do steps 5 through 8 again, crease after crease, each fold pressed forward one slant along the lattice. Never look back further than the two creases you are holding; anything older is already gone and you must not want it.

10. You are finished when the crease you have just lit holds exactly one cell — the far corner, where the last down-post crosses the last across-post. There are no further slants beyond it.

11. Read that single spark. Its color is the whole answer: the least total cost of laying the two boughs against each other, with slips allowed wherever they were needed. Let the last two creases drift off after all the others.

12. If you want the agreement told as a number rather than a cost, subtract that spark's cost from the length of the longer bough and report what remains — but the spark itself is the answer, and it never needed the sheet kept whole to burn.
