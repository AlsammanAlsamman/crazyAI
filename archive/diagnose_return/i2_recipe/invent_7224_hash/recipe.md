CHOSEN SEED: Each heap races the musk deer's mountain stride, uphill twist and downhill fold, until no heap keeps the shape it entered with.

1. Lay the marks out along the flat riverbank stone in the order they were given to you, left to right, and let the servant swat the inflated house against your mouth so you speak none of them aloud — the order on the stone is the only order there is from here on.

2. Count the marks. At the right end of the row, lay down one black pebble, then as many white pebbles as it takes to bring the row to an even count of heaps, and last, in the final heap's tail, lay pebbles spelling how many marks there were before you added any. Never skip this even when the count already comes out even — the black pebble must always be laid, or two different piles will fold to one token.

3. Cut the row into heaps of even count, walking left to right, each heap the same size as the last. The tail heap is now full by step 2; there is no short heap.

4. Set the running shape at the shrine: a fixed pattern of marks you did not choose today and will never change — the same starting shape for every pile anyone ever brings you. Write it on the shrine stone once and leave it there.

5. Take the leftmost heap that has not been raced. Set the running shape beside it. Race the pair across the mountain path in the deer's stride, and only in this stride:
   - **uphill twist:** add the heap into the running shape, position against position, carrying nothing across positions.
   - **downhill fold:** turn the whole result ninety degrees and fold its corners into its own center.
   - **doubling-back:** add the result into the shape it was before the twist, so the trail eats itself.

6. Run that three-part stride the same fixed number of strides for every heap — no fewer than eight, and the same count for every heap of every pile forever. Count them on your fingers, not by feeling that it looks finished.

7. Whatever comes off the path is the new running shape. Burn the heap at the shrine now, and burn the old running shape with it. Keep only the new running shape.

8. Return to step 5 with the next heap. Do this until no unraced heap remains on the riverbank stone.

9. Test the working before you trust it: take the original pile, change one single mark anywhere in it — the first, the middle, the last — and carry the changed pile through steps 1 through 8. Set the two final shapes side by side.

10. Look at every square of both. About half the positions must differ, scattered, with no corner or edge of the grid left the same in both. If any region survives untouched, the stride is too gentle: raise the stride count in step 6, throw both results away, and begin again at step 1 from the riverbank.

11. Repeat step 9 and 10 with at least twenty different single-mark changes at different places in the pile. Only when every one of them smears across the whole grid is the method sound.

12. You are finished when the last heap has been burned and the final running shape stands alone at the shrine — it is the token, always the same size no matter how long the pile was. Read it off the shrine stone and hand it over. The riverbank is bare; nothing remains that could walk the token back to its marks.
