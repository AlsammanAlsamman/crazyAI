CHOSEN SEED: The nightingales always land first on the stone carrying the smallest owed letter.

1. Lay out the garden as it truly is: one stone for every house, and between stones the roads that actually run, each road carrying its own thief who names a price for passage in that one direction only. Write each thief's price on the thread of his own road, and keep the two directions of a road as two separate threads with two separate prices, even where the road looks single.

2. Take the stone of the house where the traveler now stands and chalk its letter "nothing owed." Chalk every other stone in the garden with the unreadable far number — a debt too large to pay. Leave every stone unlocked.

3. Send the nightingales up over the garden. They circle only the unlocked stones and drop onto whichever carries the smallest owed letter. If every unlocked stone still wears the unreadable far number, the birds will not land at all — go to step 10.

4. Where a nightingale lands, that stone is finished. Lock its letter. It is never chalked over again, because no thief anywhere charges a negative toll, and no cheaper path can now arrive.

5. Climb to the casting tower at the locked stone and cast a thread down every road that leaves it, one thread per direction, and ask each road's thief what he is owed.

6. For each thread, add the locked stone's letter to that thief's price. This sum is what the far stone would owe if the traveler walked this way.

7. Compare that sum against the letter already chalked on the far stone. If the far stone is already locked, let the thread drop and throw it away. If the sum is not smaller than the letter already there, let the thread drop and throw it away too.

8. If the sum is smaller, scratch out the far stone's letter and chalk the smaller sum in its place — the stone has not moved a finger's width, yet its debt is stolen and rebuilt. Beside the new letter, mark the name of the locked stone the thread came from, so the road walked back can be read later.

9. Repeat from step 3: send the nightingales up again over what unlocked stones remain, land, lock, cast, compare, rebuild.

10. You are finished when the nightingales refuse to land — either because every stone is locked, or because every stone still unlocked wears the unreadable far number. Those unreadable stones are houses no road can carry the traveler to; leave them chalked as they are and throw their threads away, for the traveler was never going to reach them.

11. To walk any journey, stand at the destination stone and read the name marked beside its locked letter, then go to that stone and read its name, and so on backward until you arrive at the stone chalked "nothing owed." Reverse that walk and you have the cheapest road. The locked letter on each stone is what that whole journey costs in thieves.
