CHOSEN SEED: Every heap throws its sticks together in one land-wide throw, and the row read straight across picks each heap's shortest sliver as its new ember while the rest are burned for fuel.

1. Lay out one heap of sticks for every place in the land, each heap set down at its own place and empty at the start. At the traveler's place, set the round wooden disc etched with the single character, and on that heap lay one stick notched to nothing at all — a length of zero — and kindle it as that heap's ember. Every other heap gets cold ash and no ember, meaning its distance is still unknown.

2. Walk every road once and notch its own length into a stick kept at the roadside, so that each road holds its measure permanently and nobody has to guess it again later.

3. Begin a throw. For every road in the land at once — not one at a time — look at the heap at the road's near end. If that heap has no ember, the road does nothing this throw. If it has an ember, take a fresh stick, notch it to the ember's number added to the road's notched length, carry it down the road, and lay it on the heap at the far end.

4. When every road in the land has been walked in this way, and only then, let all heaps throw their sticks into the air together in a single land-wide throw. They settle into one pyramid: every heap's sticks crossing every other heap's sticks, one throw for the whole land.

5. Read the pyramid straight across a single row. That row holds one sliver from every heap in the same breath, so every place is judged in the same moment and none before another.

6. In each heap, compare the slivers against one another and against that heap's current ember. Keep the single shortest. If the heap had no ember, the shortest sliver becomes its ember. If the heap had an ember already and the shortest sliver is shorter than it, the sliver becomes the new ember and the old ember is burned. If no sliver is shorter, the ember stands unchanged.

7. Burn every sliver that was not kept. Feed them all into the hearths — a fire that isn't given fuel goes out, and the throws to come still need warmth. Each heap now carries exactly one thing forward: its ember, banked to a coal.

8. Note whether any ember anywhere in the land changed during this throw. If even one changed, go back to step 3 and throw again with the new embers in place.

9. If a whole throw passes and not one ember in the land changed, stop. The embers have settled and no further throw can shorten them.

10. Count the throws as you go. You may never need more throws than there are places in the land, less one. If the throws pass that count and embers still change, then somewhere a ring of roads gives back more than it takes each time around; mark every heap that keeps shrinking as unsound and do not trust its ember.

11. Look at every heap still holding cold ash and no ember after the last throw. Those places were never reached by any spark; mark each as nowhere useful — a dead field the elephants never walked — and leave its distance forever unlit.

12. Have the hooded figure read the whole land's embers aloud in one pass, place by place, each ember's notched number being the shortest way from the traveler's disc to that place, with the unlit heaps named as unreachable. That recitation is the answer.
