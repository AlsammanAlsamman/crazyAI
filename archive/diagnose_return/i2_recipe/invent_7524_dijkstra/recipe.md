CHOSEN SEED: A glint released from the traveler's jewel crawls down every cord at a pace set by the cord's own length.

1. Hang one polished jewel for every place the traveler might reach, and knot between each pair that has a road a length of blood-dark cord — the cord cut exactly as long as the road is long, so a near place hangs on a short taut cord and a far place on a long slack one. Where there is no road, knot no cord.

2. Scratch a chalk grid on the pavement beneath the net, one square per jewel, and mark each square with the name of its jewel. Leave every square blank. Set the flat throwing-stone in the square of the jewel where the traveler stands.

3. On that one square only, chalk the number nought: the stone's own square catches fire the instant you touch it, at no cost of waiting. Call every square that carries a chalked number *lit*, and every blank square *dark*.

4. Look over all the lit squares and find those whose jewel you have not yet crouched beside. Among only those, choose the one with the smallest chalked number. If two tie, take either — it makes no difference. If there are none at all, go to step 9.

5. Crouch beside that chosen jewel. Follow with your fingers every cord knotted to it, one cord at a time, and for each cord read two things: the length of the cord, and the number chalked in the chosen jewel's own square.

6. Add the cord's length to the chosen jewel's chalked number. That sum is the instant the glint would arrive at the jewel on the cord's far end by this route. Hold that sum in your hand.

7. Compare the sum against the far jewel's square. If that square is dark, chalk the sum into it — a first flare, and first light is the only light that counts. If that square already carries a number smaller than or equal to your sum, throw the sum away unmarked: it is a late flare come along a slacker cord, wasted color no dyer's cloth would keep. If the square carries a number *larger* than your sum, rub that number out and chalk the sum in its place — you had guessed a slow road before you found the quick one.

8. When every cord at the chosen jewel has been read this way, mark the chosen jewel as crouched-beside — a small stroke of chalk in the corner of its square, so you never crouch there twice — and return to step 4.

9. You are finished when step 4 finds no lit square left uncrouched, for then no glint is still travelling anywhere in the net. Now take up the chalk once more and cross out, with a single scratch, every square still dark — the way the desert crosses out its own tracks. Those places no glint ever reached; their roads lead nowhere useful, and the traveler must not be sent chasing wind.

10. Read the grid as a hopscotch child reads her thrown stone: each chalked number is the length of the shortest way from the traveler's jewel to that place, and each scratched-out square is a place with no way at all.
