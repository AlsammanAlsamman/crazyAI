CHOSEN SEED: Nightingales sent down every road sing one note per stride and die at the first board, carving that board's lattice slot with their dying note.

1. Lay out the rigid lattice of old memory first: one fixed slot for every cross-marked board in the land, no more and no fewer, each slot cut blank. Beside it lay a second, smaller lattice with the same slots, each holding one plain bead, unsettled.

2. Into the slot for the traveler's own board carve the silence — the note of no strides at all, the faintest sound there is. Leave every other slot blank.

3. Look over all slots whose bead is still unsettled and whose carving is not blank. Take the one whose carving is quietest. If no such slot exists, go to step 11.

4. Turn that slot's bead to settled. A settled bead is never turned back, and its board is never returned to.

5. Stand at that settled board. Take one nightingale for each road leaving it, and loose them all at once. Each bird sings one note per stride of road beneath it, growing hoarser with the distance, and sings itself to death at the first board its road reaches. Send no bird down a road already marked dead, and never a second bird down a road already flown.

6. At each board where a bird falls, open a cow's head. Inside, the woman milks the smaller cow: what she draws is the settled board's own carved note with that dead bird's hoarseness added onto it — the note as it sounds coming by way of the board just settled.

7. Compare her milk against what is already carved in that fallen board's slot. If the slot is blank, or if her milk is thinner than the carving, scrape the slot clean and cut her milk in its place. If her milk is the same or fatter, spill it in the desert and leave the carving as it stands.

8. Where two dying notes reach the same board, this comparing has already kept the fainter and let the louder rot — keep nothing else. What's thrown away stays thrown away.

9. Mark dead any road whose bird brings nothing thinner and whose far board is already settled, and send no bird down it again.

10. Return to step 3.

11. You are finished when no unsettled slot holds a carving — no nightingale is left to sing and no cow yields a thinner milk. Every reachable board bears its one permanent carving: the shortest way there. Every slot still blank is a place past reach, blank as the pink-brown desert with no landmark, and its roads led nowhere useful at all.

12. Read the traveler the whole lattice in order, slot by slot: each board, its carved note, and for the blank ones, the desert.
