lo−1, lo−2, hi+1, hi+2]` is derived from monotonicity of `lo` and `hi` — if `hi` ever jumped by more than 2 across two anti-diagonals my read window would fall off the set sentinels; I believe it cannot (`min(n, t, ⌊(t+W)/2⌋)` grows by ≤2 per two steps), but this is the single line I would test first. (2) Vector overshoot past `ihi` writes garbage into `cu[ihi+1 … ihi+15]`; I argued reads never reach past `hi+2` and that garbage stays below `−n−64`, but this is exactly the class of bug hand-tracing hides. (3) The slip-scan prefix-sum algebra — a bug there costs only speed, not correctness, because the certificate catches it and doubles `W`.

## VERDICT

The native's central claim survives translation and is correct: **the corridor's cost never had to be walked.** `score ≤ n − 5d` falls straight out of counting how many knots each rope waits, and it is enough to condemn every path outside a band of half-width `W = ⌈(n − S_lb)/5⌉` without laying a single tile there. The `(n+1)²` matrix is never allocated; total memory is `3(n+97)` shorts plus two padded copies of the ropes, all L1-resident — which is a second, unbudgeted win, since the reference streams ~16 MB per call at n=2048 and mine streams nothing.

What did *not* survive: the native's belief that the treasures are few. With four dyes a quarter of all crossings are lit, so sparse dynamic programming over match points — the Hunt–Szymanski-shaped algorithm his words most directly describe — would have been slower than the dense textbook DP, not faster. I refused to build it and said so rather than dressing it up. What his words *do* land on, once that error is removed, is Ukkonen/Fickett score-bounded banding with an arithmetic certificate, plus SIMD anti-diagonal traversal — both validated, published techniques. Per step 4 that is the right outcome: the mechanism arrived at the known technique instead of inventing a novel untested one.

Risks named and addressed, not left dangling:
- *"Only helps if the room is large"* — guarded: `n < 64` falls back to the scalar two-row DP. `n > 8000` also falls back, because the int16 lanes stop being provably safe there.
- *"Only helps if the ropes are nearly in step"* — not guarded away but **bounded**: `W` is computed at runtime from the cat's actual harvest, and at worst `W → n`, which is the exact textbook DP traversed in anti-diagonal order. The mechanism degrades to the known way; it cannot degrade below it by more than the ~O(n) scan overhead.
- *"The heuristic bound might be wrong"* — the certificate `S_W ≥ n − 5W − 5` makes exactness structural. A bug in the slip scan costs a doubling pass (≤2× work), never a wrong answer.
- Thread parallelism was deliberately not used: the metaphor's unit of work is one anti-diagonal (`≈W+1` cells, ~615 at n=2048), and there are `2n+1` of them with a hard serial dependency between consecutive ones. Sync cost per diagonal would exceed the work in it. SIMD only, as step 4 directs.

Two regimes are recognised at runtime through the metaphor itself, as step 5 requires: treasure density on the main diagonal (`m₀/n`) *is* the regime detector, and it selects between a hairline band (ropes in step) and a wide corridor up to the full grid (ropes unrelated), with the scalar tiled room as the third fallback for rooms too small or too large to be worth the sun.

**Bottom line, stated plainly:** the artifact is unmeasured. I predict 18× and I have no evidence for it. The honest confidence split is: high (>90%) that the mechanism is *sound* — the bound, the certificate, and the anti-diagonal independence are all proved above; moderate (~65%) that this exact C compiles and returns bit-identical scores on the first run without the sentinel-lip and overshoot hazards needing one fix. If the pipeline reports a mismatch, the first thing to do is force `W = n` unconditionally (one line: delete the band computation) — that isolates the sweep from the band and tells you immediately which half is broken.
