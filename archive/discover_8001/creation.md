The Tide-Clock of Armada

Lashed into the westernmost hull-joint of Armada, where three drifting districts hinge together and groan, there is a clock that keeps no hour at all — it keeps *debt*. It is a wheel of forty-one ships' bells, salvaged from vessels that sank before they were bound into the city, hung concentric on a mast of fused anchor-chain. Each bell is tuned not to a note but to a grief: the bell from a bride-ship that never landed rings flattest, the bell from a plague-hulk rings so high it draws blood from the ear if you stand beneath it too long.

The mechanism is the sea-beasts themselves. Their chains, the same ones that haul Armada's districts across the water, run up through the deck and turn the wheel as the beasts pull — so the clock does not mark time, it marks *distance travelled while owing something*. The harder the beasts strain, the faster the bells turn, and the citizens have learned to read the rhythm the way sailors read swell: a slow toll means the city is drifting through calm, unclaimed water and nobody need pay attention. A fast, stuttering toll means Armada is crossing a place where an old wreck's ghosts still claim the water as theirs, and every household hangs a lily from its window until the ringing slows again — because a single lily, dropped wrong, can knock a customs clerk flat, and nobody wants that on a stuttering day.

No one built it to tell anyone anything useful. It was built because forty-one bells recovered from forty-one deaths made an unbearable silence in the hold, and someone could not stand it, and hung them where the chains already moved. Now the whole city sets its weeping-hour by it, and the barbershops go quiet exactly when the wheel does.
