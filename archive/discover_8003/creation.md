I built the Kinship Knot to answer a question the pole-walkers pretend not to ask: when a thing has changed all the way through, what is left to call it the same thing?

Take the pole first — you know it, the one that stands for nothing and holds up everything. I tie one end of a plain cord to its foot. The other end I loop, loose, around the collarbone of whichever figure has just finished a transformation so complete that no cell of the old self remains. Not tight. A collar that could fall off if the wearer forgot to breathe carefully. That's the whole mechanism: a leash between a support that does nothing and a body that has become someone else.

Here is what it does. Walk away from the pole and the cord draws taut at three steps — same as the pole's own rule, borrowed, because I didn't see why falling should need two different arithmetics. But the Knot adds a second reading the pole alone never gave: at the exact moment the cord goes taut, the wearer's shadow splits into two dark, motionless figures, standing where the missile-turned-stone-step used to be, joined to each other by a straight line like a fold pressed into the sky. Those two never argue about which one is real. They just stand there being proof — proof that the storm insists on its own continuity even after every belief that built it has burned off and been replaced by different weather entirely.

It is made of the cord, the pole, and one more part I'm proudest of: a small creature I keep near the base, half rung-ladder, half held note, that hums a single pitch whenever the taut cord trembles. Not a warning. A witness-sound. It has an ear where a ladder shouldn't have one, and legs where a note has no business walking, and it uses both badly, charmingly, to pace back and forth along the cord's length checking tension.

What I noticed, watching brides and grooms and the freshly molted pass through it on the day after their weddings, the day after everything: the cord always goes slack a half-second before they reach the grass. In that slack second they are closer to the dirt and to their own dying than to any name they used to answer to. That's not a flaw in the Knot. That's the only honest thing it measures.
