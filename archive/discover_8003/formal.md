What’s described has a recognizable skeleton, though a few of its pieces are irreducibly interpretive and won't fold into a single clean formula. Reporting both parts honestly:

**The formal skeleton (this part does reduce cleanly):**

1. **A null anchor and a mutable subject, joined by a bounded tether.** One endpoint is defined only by function, not content ("stands for nothing"); the other endpoint is a body that has undergone total internal replacement — none of its original constituents survive the transformation, yet it is still treated as *one* referent. The tether has slack up to a fixed critical distance *r*, then goes rigid. This is a constraint function: inert for d < r, active at d = r — a threshold/boundary condition, indifferent to *what* the subject has become, sensitive only to *how far* it has moved.

2. **A bifurcation exactly at the threshold.** At d = r, the single subject's projection (its shadow, its lower-dimensional trace) doesn't just stop — it splits into two static, mutually rigid copies. One event, one boundary crossing, produces a symmetric pair joined by an invariant relation. This is offered as the system's evidence of continuity: not "the subject kept its properties" (it didn't — that's the premise) but "the boundary event itself reliably generates the same twofold structure." Continuity is relocated from the content of the thing to the shape of the event that happens *to* it.

3. **A derivative-coupled witness, categorically unclassifiable.** A third component monitors not the constraint's state but its *rate of change* — it responds to tremor in the tether, not to tautness itself, and it emits without asserting (a signal, not a verdict). It's built as a hybrid of two things that don't belong in the same category (a measuring/support structure and an expressive/tonal one), which seems to be the point: the monitor must sit outside both the anchor's category and the subject's category to be a neutral witness rather than a party to either.

4. **An asymptotic decoupling just short of the boundary.** The one irregular, non-tidy element: the constraint releases *before* geometric contact, in a vanishing interval adjacent to but not at the limit. In that gap, the quantity apparently being tracked changes referent — it stops indexing "distance from the assigned name" and starts indexing "distance from a base/undifferentiated state." The apparatus built to test whether a label survives transformation, at the last possible moment, admits it was measuring something else the whole time — proximity to a zero-state, not fidelity to an identity.

**Where this resists full reduction:** steps 2 and 4 aren't just structural — they carry a smuggled-in *interpretation* ("this doubling counts as proof," "this gap is the honest measurement") that isn't derivable from the constraint mechanics alone. You could formalize the anchor–tether–threshold as a boundary-value system, and the bifurcation as a pitchfork-type event triggered by crossing r rather than by a drifting parameter — but the claim that the bifurcation is *evidence* and that the pre-boundary slack is *the honest reading* are value judgments riding on top of the geometry, not consequences of it. So: a real, describable structure — anchor/tether/threshold/bifurcation/witness — wrapped around a non-formal residue that has to be stated as interpretation, not derived as consequence.
