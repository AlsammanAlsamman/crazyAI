I keep coming back to the Doorturn, so let me tell it properly.

Behind is a city of ships lashed together, decks for streets, and no ship keeps the same neighbor for long — the whole tangle drifts on its ropes. So a door that opens onto a friend's gangway this season would open onto black water the next, if nothing corrected it. The Doorturn corrects it.

It is a cuffed ring set into the doorframe, loose enough to flap, tight enough to hold a count. Every crown that changes hands in Behind — won, stripped, gambled in a tavern whose name the shadows keep — notches the ring once, on every door in the city, all at the same moment, the way a tide lifts every hull together. When the notches complete a full turn, the door swings to face whatever deck has drifted nearest, and latches there for the season.

The elephant is the reason it can never be predicted from outside: it strips a crown from one hand and is already leaning toward the next before the first door has finished hatching its turn, so the count is always mid-motion, always owed one more notch than anyone can see. That's the rule underneath the rule — the city's roads are not a map, they are an arrears.

What I like about the Doorturn is that it never fully closes the account. A door always owes the tally something. That's why the streets keep being roads at all, instead of settling into a shape and dying there.
