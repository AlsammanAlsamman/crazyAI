Looking at this dispassionately — the composite doesn't reduce to one formal object (you said so yourself, correctly), so I looked for real problems that match *individual pieces or piece-clusters* cleanly rather than forcing the whole five-piece bundle onto one target. Three came out real; I'll also flag what I checked and rejected.

**1. Epistemic self-knowledge puzzles (muddy children / iterated public announcement) — pieces 1 + 4**

*Application:* The muddy-children puzzle has exactly your core: one shared global fact (the count *k* of muddy children) and *N* agents each seeing an offset projection of it (everyone else's status, not their own). No agent can privately "edit" their apparent knowledge — it's fixed by the shared count plus their fixed vantage. Critically, piece 4 also holds exactly: an agent's own status is only derivable after a specific number of rounds of public announcement that amounts to *traversing everyone else's information first* — self-knowledge sits at the end of an ordered path through others' read-outs, not addressable directly. This isn't analogy, it's the same logical skeleton, just formalized already in epistemic logic (Kripke models, common knowledge operators).

*How to check:* Build the Kripke model for *N* agents with your structure's actual offsets (not just muddy/clean but a continuum of "identity values" as in piece 2), and verify whether an inverse-map/decode condition needs to be *reached* (common knowledge of round *r*) before an agent's own value becomes derivable, exactly at round *N*−1. If it holds for the generalized (continuum-valued, not binary) version, you've got a genuine generalization of the muddy-children result, not just a restatement — that's a checkable, publishable formal claim, not a vibe.

**2. "Cache becomes the source of truth" bug pattern in distributed systems — piece 3 only**

*Application:* This is a real, well-known failure class: a derived/indirect channel (read replica, search index, CDN edge cache, materialized view) that answers *faster* than the canonical store, and which client code ends up trusting as authoritative precisely because it's first and easy — even though it's provably derived and can be stale or wrong relative to the primary. Your structure's "mirror leaks the value before the direct-angle read, and gets treated as authoritative" is a one-to-one description of this anti-pattern, right down to the asymmetry in required conditions (direct read needs a consistency-respecting query path; the cache needs nothing) and in arrival time.

*How to check:* This is empirically testable with existing tooling — Jepsen-style linearizability testing against a system with both a canonical store and a faster derived channel. The falsifiable claim your structure suggests: bugs cluster specifically around the case where the derived channel's *timestamp* precedes the canonical commit's visibility, not just around staleness in general. That's a narrower, checkable hypothesis than "caches can be stale," and it's the kind of thing you could mine from existing incident postmortems (search for "cache was ahead of," "read-after-write," "stale index served before commit").

**3. Shortcut learning in deep nets — piece 5 only**

*Application:* Piece 5's normative claim — that a condition producing the *same output* through a different route ("glare") is treated as epistemically lesser than decode-via-narrow-condition-plus-traversal — is exactly the empirical distinction the shortcut-learning literature (Geirhos et al., "Shortcut Learning in Deep Neural Networks," 2020) draws between models that rely on spurious/shortcut features (e.g., texture, background) versus intended/causal features (shape, structure). Same training accuracy, same output — different status, because one is fragile.

*How to check:* This one's already checked, repeatedly, in the literature — cue-conflict stimuli (Stylized-ImageNet), distribution-shift test sets. The structural claim your piece 5 makes is falsifiable in exactly that paradigm: does the "glare" route degrade under conditions that don't disturb the "legitimate" route? If yes, the normative ranking isn't just rhetorical, it predicts a real, measured asymmetry in robustness.
