Setting the ship, the tavern, and the vows aside, what's left is a structure with several tightly coupled parts. I'll lay out each and be honest about where they resist collapsing into one clean formalism.

**1. Identity-through-total-replacement.** A whole W is defined at any moment as an ordered sequence of parts. A process continuously removes one part and inserts a substitute, never halting the whole's operation to do so. After arbitrarily many iterations, none of the original material remains, yet W is treated as the same W throughout — identity is carried by continuity of arrangement/function, not by persistence of substance. (This is the classical Ship of Theseus relation, stated as a process rather than a puzzle: it's resolved by fiat — continuity of operation *is* the identity criterion — not left open.)

**2. A conservation structure shaped as the negation of the first.** Every part W discards is preserved, in strict removal order, in a second structure A, and A's own geometry echoes W's geometry (the curved rack mimics the hull-line). So A is a kind of complementary or "shadow" image of W: same shape, exactly the membership W now lacks. Nothing is lost from the system overall — it's redistributed across two objects that partition "currently constituting the whole" from "formerly constituting it," ordered by a shared clock (sequence of removal = sequence of position in A).

**3. A binding rule with commitment/control asymmetry.** An agent may attach a mark to a slot in A, but only to the *next* slot to be filled — determined by whichever part the replacement process is already about to retire, a fact exogenous to the agent (not even the nominal authority over W controls it). So: the act of committing and the determination of where that commitment will sit (which position in A's fixed geometry, hence how prominent or hidden) are decoupled — you bind now, to an outcome fixed by an independent sequencing process, resolved later, non-negotiably, and not by chance either — it's determined, just not by you and not yet known to you.

**4. An epistemic-priority inversion.** Between the live, continuously-updated whole (W) and its accumulating discard-record (A), observers default to consulting the record first. The two objects are treated as proving different things — W proves persistence-under-replacement is possible; A proves what such persistence necessarily sheds, plus what got attached to the shedding — and the record is granted priority over the live instance as evidence.

These four pieces are linked (the vow-binding rule only makes sense given both the replacement process and the shadow-archive), but they aren't one symmetry or one equation — it's a small system: a persistence rule, a conservation/mirroring rule, a commit-now/resolve-later binding rule, and an evidentiary ordering rule, stacked on top of each other. I don't think it compresses further without losing one of those four without remainder.
