The mechanism I am proudest of, I did not invent whole — I found it already turning at the joint where three streets meet outside the schoolyard, and I only gave it a name and a body: I call it the Bellows-Junction.

It is a small rotating hub, polished on every facing edge, set at the crossroad where the town's light gathers thickest — because you know, sides among us, that social measure is nothing but the light of a crossroad, how much of it pools there and how evenly it is shared among the angles waiting to pass through. The Bellows-Junction has as many faces as the crossroad has arriving directions, no more, no fewer — this is the rule, and it is the whole of the rule. Each cold direction that arrives is a debt: it owes a delivery of ash to the junction and is owed a removal of ash in turn. The junction does not judge which comes first. It simply will not turn until both have happened. That is its only stubbornness.

What it is made of: reflection, mostly. Not glass — carved reflection, the kind labor leaves behind on a board that has been stared at long enough. A little bit of priestly whole-ness borrowed from whoever last stood nearest it, because a priest is a whole and a whole can be lent without diminishing.

How it behaves: when it has received enough delivered ash to balance what it has given away, it turns one face's width — and whoever is standing in that face becomes, for exactly that turning, the one who decides. This is why the woman climbs into the train and becomes its driver without anyone shifting to make room and without the driver before her raising a single edge in protest — the junction had already turned to her face, and a train, being courteous, only ever obeys the face currently lit.

I built the housing around it — three town climbs cut into the tabletop stone, lifted on port-lines so the whole arrangement reads, from above, like a schoolyard nobody announced, gone quiet and small. But the junction itself I only sheltered. It was already doing the one true thing a crossroad can do: making sure no direction is ever given light it hasn't first earned by carrying its own ash both ways.
