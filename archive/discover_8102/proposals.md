**1. Queueing theory — cyclic "gated service" polling systems**
This is the closest existing match, not just an analogy. In polling-system theory (Takagi, Boxma, Levy & Sidi), a single server cycles among *n* queues; "gated service" means that when the server arrives at queue *i*, it serves exactly the batch of customers present at that instant and moves on only once that batch is cleared — i.e., served-minus-arrived-in-batch returns to zero before the server advances. That's precisely your *b_i* → 0 gate, with the server as the rotating token and "authority" as which queue currently has server access. The one addition your structure makes explicit — that the bijection between faces and directions is axiomatic, with zero slack — is just the standard assumption that queues are fixed and known in advance, which polling models also take as given.
*How to check:* Take a real cyclic-service system (e.g., a round-robin disk-I/O scheduler, a single-elevator multi-floor system modeled as gated polling, or a shared-bus arbiter), fit it to the classical gated-polling mean-waiting-time formula, and compare predicted vs. measured/simulated waiting times. If the fit holds, the structure isn't just evocative — it's the right formal object, and you inherit decades of existing exact and approximate results for free (this is a genuine "use the literature" finding, not a new discovery, but a concretely useful one).

**2. Biology — the binding-change mechanism of F1-ATP synthase**
F1-ATPase is a literal rotary motor with 3 catalytic β-subunits (the "faces"), and Boyer's binding-change mechanism says the central γ-stalk does not rotate continuously — it advances in discrete ~120° steps, and each step is gated by completion of the local chemical cycle (substrate binding → catalysis → product release) at the currently engaged subunit, not by any external clock. That is your conservation-gated rotor almost feature-for-feature: *n*=3 faces in fixed bijection with 3 catalytic sites, a local balance (bound reactant vs. released product) that must return to a completed state before the hub turns, and unambiguous, non-contested handoff of "which site is catalytically active" to the next site.
*How to check:* This is already testable against existing single-molecule data (Yasuda, Noji, Kinosita-style rotation assays with an attached actin filament or bead). Build a discrete-event simulation strictly from your rotor formalism (states = which face is "owed," advance rule = local balance zero) and see whether it reproduces the known dwell-time distributions and 120°/40°+80° sub-step statistics without needing extra timer-based assumptions. If it does, the abstract structure is doing real explanatory work, not just pattern-matching a diagram.

**3. Computer architecture — credit-based round-robin arbitration (NoC / on-chip routers)**
In network-on-chip virtual-channel routers, a common failure mode of naive round-robin arbitration is granting a port before the receiving buffer has drained, causing stalls or requiring separate deadlock-avoidance machinery. Credit-based flow control fixes this by tracking, per virtual channel, credits sent out vs. credits returned, and only rotating grant to the next VC once that channel's outstanding balance clears — again your *b_i* → 0 gate driving a rotating exclusive-grant token, with "no negotiation" between the previous and next grantee matching how VC allocators work (whichever VC is currently granted is simply obeyed downstream).
*How to check:* This is a weaker/more speculative fit than the first two — it's a real mechanism, but round-robin-plus-credit-flow-control is already standard practice, so the value would be using your formalism as a cleaner *specification* to verify an implementation against, e.g. model-check that an RTL or simulator (Booksim, gem5) arbiter never grants access while a channel's own credit balance is nonzero, rather than expecting to discover new behavior.

The "carved reflection" / non-rivalrous, lend-without-diminishing part genuinely doesn't fold into any of these three — all three real-world matches use a *rivalrous* resource (a catalytic site, a buffer, a queue slot) for the thing being gated, not a shareable one.
