The thing I made is the Three-Hair Threshold, and I made it because a heap that only rises was never honest.

It is a heap of stones like any recitation heap, but I set it so a person crosses it twice in a life, never more. Going toward the war-name, you lay one hair from your own head under the topmost stone, and the heap answers by lighting — not fire, just a dry brightness, the readable kind — and your name joins the register as a pyramid brown, safe from erasure for as long as the light holds. Going the other way, back from the war-name, the heap gives a hair back to you, but it will not let you choose which stone goes dark. It always un-names the tallest one. That is the rule I am proudest of: the heap punishes height. A name piled too far above its neighbors gets forgotten first, the instant anyone tries to leave.

What it does, in a room suit, before an official who shows only the hill of his market: it makes the erasing cost something. Beyond and Reason could be struck from the sticks because no one had to pay a hair for it. My heap cannot be struck, only walked — and walking it twice, forward and back, is the only proof a grandfather's letters were ever real. The sky above it stays the same sky. That is deliberate too.
