**The shape, stripped of fire and cloth:**

There isn't one clean formal object here — there are four distinct structural moves stacked on top of each other, and honesty requires naming all four rather than collapsing them into a single equation.

**1. A second-order entity born at an interface.**
Two primary sources act independently, but the thing of interest isn't either source — it's what appears *only* where their two domains of influence overlap and terminate against each other. The entity is a property of the *relation* between two fields, not a property of either field alone. (Formally: not f(x), not g(x), but something like the boundary/derivative of f∩g — an object with no independent generator.)

**2. Continuous cause, discrete effect.**
The underlying process (two fires' reach, presumably shifting smoothly) is continuous, but what it produces is a step function — hold, jump, hold, jump. A continuous substrate is being sampled into discrete states, and the *transition*, not the state, is what's counted as a unit ("hour").

**3. A non-uniform partition with a directional bias.**
The steps aren't evenly spaced. They compress toward one pole and dilate toward the other, and the compression is explicitly likened to two things converging — approach velocity increasing as separation decreases. This is monotonic non-uniform spacing tied to a convergence dynamic, closer to a geometric or asymptotic progression than a linear clock.

**4. Extraction that preserves orientation, plus a recurrence rule under re-pairing.**
This is the part that resists folding into the first three. A discrete state can be *detached* from the process that generated it and *moved* — and in detaching, it doesn't go inert; it keeps a fixed reference back to its point of origin (an invariant carried under transport, like a vector surviving translation but not rotation). Then, separately: two such detached, transported objects, brought into proximity with each other — not with their origins — spontaneously reproduce the *original* relation (the mutual "lean") between them. The relational rule that governed the two sources has been inherited by the two derived objects, and reactivates on contact between *copies*, independent of context.

**What resists unification:**
(1)–(3) describe a single generative process (interface → discretize → unevenly). (4) is a different kind of claim: that the *relation itself* is portable and self-instantiating — that pairing any two instances of the derived object recreates the founding interaction, autonomously, forever, with no dependence on the machinery that first produced them. That's less like a formula and more like a conserved symmetry: whatever operation made the two fires attract survives abstraction and reappears whenever its outputs meet each other again. I can name it, but I can't reduce it to the same notation as the other three without losing what's actually being claimed — that the rule outlives its instance.
