**1. SRE/observability design — alert-channel contamination and canary health checks**

The mapping is close to literal, not decorative. Real distributed systems already do part 1: canaries/health-checks attached one-to-one to each service, "calibrated" by running against actual production traffic patterns (not synthetic load — synthetic canaries notoriously miss real failure modes, which is exactly the "a dish can't substitute for in-situ contact" clause), reporting continuous graded golden-signal metrics (p50/p99 latency, error rate) rather than a binary up/down, aggregated on one dashboard so an on-call engineer can localize which service is degrading before it hard-fails. Part 2 is also a documented, named failure mode: "alert fatigue" — once a paging channel is trusted, routing unrelated/non-actionable noise into it (a flaky low-priority check, a cron job's routine warning) gets misread as a real signal, and because the channel carries earned trust, the overreaction (paging the whole team at 3am) is disproportionate to the actual event. SRE practice already has an informal rule matching the text's rule exactly: keep paging channels monopolized by actionable signal; route everything else elsewhere.

*How to check:* pull an org's incident/postmortem history and alert logs. Compute, per service, the fraction of alerts on a channel that were actionable ("signal purity") and correlate it with mean time-to-acknowledge and false-escalation rate. If channels with lower purity show slower or more erratic response to *real* incidents (engineers desensitized by noise), that's direct evidence the contamination-rule half of the structure is load-bearing, not just a metaphor. Separately, for the sensing half: check whether canaries calibrated on real production traffic have measurably longer failure-prediction lead time than synthetic/generic canaries on the same services — that's a clean before/after or A/B comparison already loggable in most APM systems.

**2. Structural crack/creep "tell-tale" monitoring in civil and mining engineering**

This is the source domain in disguise, but it's worth stating as a real, checkable engineering claim rather than just noting the resemblance. The structure specifies design constraints that aren't always respected in practice: the indicator must be glued/fixed directly to the specific crack or roof stratum it reports on (a witness plate calibrated on a nearby but different crack is useless — specificity-by-contact), must report a graded quantity (degree of offset/discoloration) not a pass/fail snap, must be read jointly across all monitored points from one vantage (an inspection walk or a monitoring board) so the *worst* point is found before any visible sag or fall, and the sensors must hang/sit free of load so they don't become structural themselves and bias what they measure.

*How to check:* this is directly testable against existing mine-roof extensometer and building crack-monitor datasets. Take historical roof-fall or wall-failure incidents where tell-tale readings exist in the log; check whether the graded reading trend (rate of change, not just final value) gave lead time before the failure was otherwise visible, and whether false alarms in the record correlate with something unrelated sharing the same inspection field of view (debris, unrelated staining, a second crack crossing the monitored one) — i.e., whether the channel-contamination failure mode actually shows up as a real category of false positive in inspection records, not just a hypothetical.
