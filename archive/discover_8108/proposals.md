**1. Primary/witness failover in distributed systems (lease-based leader election)**

How it applies: A leader emits heartbeats/lease-renewal pulses (the "clicks") rather than exposing its actual internal state directly to followers — followers gate their behavior on *hearing the pulse*, not on directly verifying the underlying condition. The classic asymmetric failure matches real lease semantics exactly: if the leader's actions outrun its lease signal (acts as leader after the lease should have been renewed but wasn't yet re-confirmed), the *cluster* can look calm while the leader itself is no longer "whole" (split-brain risk — its writes may be later disowned). If the lease pulse arrives but no corresponding state change follows (heartbeat with no real progress), nothing breaks — the system just stalls waiting, which is the safe direction. The witness/standby node matches this well too: it sits passively verifying only that the leader's heartbeat is *honest* (well-formed, on schedule), indifferent to what the leader is actually deciding — until the leader's own lease self-expires (loses its exclusive claim to being "the" leader, i.e., loses self-referential grip on its identity as leader), at which point the witness converts from passive verifier to active duplicate participant (promotes itself and starts operating in parallel). This is structurally close to Raft/ZooKeeper-style lease election with a warm-standby witness.

How to check: Implement a toy lease-based leader election (e.g., in a simulator with injectable clock skew). Verify the predicted asymmetry empirically: (a) skewing the leader's action-clock *ahead* of its lease-renewal signal should produce silent correctness violations (duplicate/conflicting writes, i.e., "not whole") without an obvious external alarm; (b) skewing it *behind* should only produce liveness delay (stalls, re-elections) with no safety violation. If both directions instead produce symmetric failures, the mapping is wrong.

**2. Clock-domain-crossing (CDC) / handshake logic in asynchronous digital circuits — partial match**

How it applies: Points 1–4 alone (bounded sample → discrete pulse → pulse-gated state advance → direction-dependent desync failure) are a near-literal description of a request/acknowledge handshake between two clock domains. The gate reads the toggle/pulse, never the underlying data value directly. Setup-violation-style desync (data changing too close to/ahead of the sampling edge) causes metastability or corrupted-but-unflagged data — a "calm sea" externally, broken data internally. Hold-violation-style desync (signal arrives, data hasn't changed yet) typically just costs a cycle of latency, no corruption — matching "gate idles, no punishment."

Where it stops applying: points 5–6 (a second circuit passively monitoring *honesty of the pulse train itself*, then converting to full active parallel operation once the primary loses some self-referential internal state) don't have a standard counterpart in CDC synchronizer design — I can't point to a real circuit pattern that does this without inventing one, so I'm flagging this mapping as covering only half the structure.

How to check: Build a small synchronous/asynchronous FIFO testbench in an HDL simulator, deliberately inject setup-violating vs. hold-violating skew on the write/read pointer handshake, and confirm the predicted asymmetry (silent corruption vs. harmless stall) actually shows up in simulation.

I didn't find a third mapping I'd stand behind — candidates I considered (STDP timing windows in neuroscience, reference-counted garbage collection) fit points 1–4 or 5–6 individually but not the whole structure, so I'm leaving it at two rather than padding to three.
