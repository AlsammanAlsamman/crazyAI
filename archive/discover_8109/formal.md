Setting the imagery aside, what's left is a composite of three distinct formal patterns, threaded together but not fused into one:

**1. An integrate-and-fire (threshold-accumulation) process, with the accumulating medium decoupled from the site of effect.**
A generator repeats one invariant local action; each repetition sheds one discrete unit. Units land on a receiving substrate that is defined by *not* changing state in response to input — an absorbing boundary with zero visible feedback, no matter the load. Meanwhile a separate counter tracks the *cardinality* of what's landed, and when that count crosses a fixed threshold, it fires a discrete, qualitative event in a third system entirely unconnected to the substrate. So: input accumulates in one place, the response happens in another place, and the substrate's stillness is precisely what lets the counting be the only thing that matters — quantity substitutes for any visible change of state.

**2. A fixed-order two-term transition where the second term is not a new state but the relaxed limit of the first.**
The triggered event resolves as an ordered pair, irreversible in sequence, where term two is defined *as* term one once its driving tension (fear, holding) has finished discharging. Not two independent states linked by a rule, but one state and its own exhausted form — asymmetry built from decay rather than from difference.

**3. A degeneracy resolved only by external, post-hoc perturbation — not by inspection.**
Among the accumulated units, which are structurally uniform and outwardly indistinguishable, only one is actually load-bearing; the rest are inert despite identical construction. This isn't detectable by looking — it requires an indifferent external probe applying a resonance test (a swept excitation, checking for response) after the accumulation is already complete. The functional unit is identified by responding; the others are retroactively confirmed as always having been non-functional, a fact true of them all along but establishable only by that later act of probing.

None of these three collapses into the other two. (1) is a dynamics-of-accumulation structure — closest to a leaky/non-leaky integrator crossing a threshold. (2) is a small asymmetric relation, almost trivial in isolation. (3) is an epistemic/structural claim about hidden functional heterogeneity within apparent uniformity, resolvable only by dynamic testing rather than static description. The thing that's genuinely interesting is that all three are made to depend on the *same* accumulated set — the pile that doesn't move the water is also the pile that, sampled later, turns out to have exactly one live member. That coupling — an accumulation that is simultaneously counted for a threshold effect *and* differentiated for a hidden-cause effect — is the actual structure, and I don't have a single clean formalism that carries both jobs at once without seams. So: three legible pieces, deliberately overlaid on one substrate, not one shape.
