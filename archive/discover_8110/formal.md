What's described has a fairly clean invariant shape, even though it's dressed in very particular imagery. Stripped down:

**1. A flow between an excessive source and a tolerant consumer.**
Something generates more intensity than the downstream can safely use — a pressure, a charge, a force building at one end of a line and needing to reach the other end in usable form.

**2. A regulator inserted mid-line, defined by a discrete parameter.**
Not a valve with continuous adjustment, but a *counted* intervention — a fixed number of "crossings," bites, or increments — that converts a continuous quantity into something that can be specified, repeated, and taught.

**3. A narrow admissible band bounded by two symmetric failure modes.**
Too little regulation → starvation/underflow (the house gets nothing, the grief drowns the family). Too much → overflow/collapse into a different kind of ruin (the lamp burns white and dies, something in the house goes hollow). The correct value isn't "more is safer" — it's a single interior point, flanked on both sides by failure, not a threshold you can round toward.

**4. That interior point is found empirically, not derived.**
It's arrived at by embodied trial and error against real failures ("ruining lamps until my hands remembered") rather than by calculation. The rule (seven, in this telling) is a *discovered constant*, not a designed one — competence precedes formula.

**5. The same regulating competence turns out to be portable across two outwardly unrelated instances of the problem.**
Two domains that share nothing in their surface material (rope fiber under mechanical/electrical stress; hair-cutting bound up with a family's grief) turn out to be the *same* problem at the structural level — "reduce an excess to a livable amount, using a hand trained to feel where the interior point is." The practitioners of one already possess the skill for the other, because the skill was never actually about the substance; it was about calibrating attenuation itself.

So the general structure is something like: **two instances of a bounded titration problem — excess flowing from a source, a discrete regulator with a single correct interior setting between two opposite failure states, that setting found empirically and then fixed into a teachable procedure — turn out to be secretly the same problem, solvable by the same transferable, substance-independent skill.**

Where it resists full formalization: the *reason* the two domains turn out isomorphic isn't argued, only asserted by juxtaposition — it's a claim about kinship of skill (the trained hand) rather than a proof of structural identity between "grief" and "current." That link is poetic, not derivable from the rest of the structure, and I'd be overstating it to pretend otherwise.
