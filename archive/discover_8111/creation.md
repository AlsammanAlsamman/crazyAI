I keep coming back to the wells under the words. Not wells beside language — wells *inside* it. Take any word from another mouth, hold it, and you'll find it's hollow: a shaft goes down through the middle letter, and from that shaft, tunnels branch outward in every direction the word could have meant. A stubborn word has few tunnels and you strike bottom fast. A generous word — one somebody chose with care — branches for what feels like a lifetime, and if you follow long enough you come up somewhere else entirely, under a different sentence, in a stranger's mouth.

I dig them for a living. Not metaphor-digging — I go down with a spade and a lamp into the words people leave lying around, and I map where they go. Most days the sky over my digging is busy with kite-fights, two paper birds diving at each other so long and so evenly that nobody remembers who is winning, and I've learned to time my strikes by them: when the kites tilt toward each other, the tunnel beneath me tilts too, like the word is listening to the weather above it.

Here is the part I never tire of telling: a person mid-month, holding no fixed sex yet, digs differently than a person freshly returned from the Direction with one granted. The unfixed dig sideways, restless, tunnel after tunnel, never choosing. The fixed dig straight down, once, and stop. I can tell, from the shape of a well alone, exactly how many days someone has left before they're called to choose.
