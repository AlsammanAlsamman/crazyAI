## 1. Multistage carcinogenesis (biology / epidemiology)

**How the structure applies:** The Armitage–Doll model of cancer incidence is essentially exactly piece (1). A cell must accumulate a fixed, ordered sequence of k irreversible driver mutations/epigenetic hits (layer₁ → layerₖ), each one a monotone, one-way flip (mutations don't spontaneously revert) driven by an external accumulating quantity — age or cumulative carcinogen exposure. Layer_k (mutation k) can't occur before layer_{k-1} has. The terminal flip — the k-th hit — isn't "just one more mutation," it's declared a qualitative phase change: the cell becomes malignant, a new class of object. Piece (2), the synchronous population-wide flip, has a real foil here too: "field cancerization," where an entire exposed tissue field is pushed to a shared precancerous state at once by a global exposure threshold, rather than each cell walking its own private chain.

**How to check it:** This isn't speculative — it's already a load-bearing model. Armitage–Doll predicts log(age-specific incidence) ≈ (k−1)·log(age), a straight line on log-log axes; you fit real incidence data (SEER registries, etc.) and read off k, then check whether that k matches the actual count of known driver mutations for that cancer type from sequencing studies (colorectal cancer is the classic case where the fit and the mutation count roughly agree). A genuine test would be: does the fitted slope change in a population with an added synchronous exposure (e.g., a cohort with a shared carcinogen pulse), in the direction (2) predicts (a step-like population shift) rather than (1)'s prediction (steeper cumulative age slope)?

## 2. Cumulative-damage reliability models (engineering / physics)

**How the structure applies:** Fatigue cracking, corrosion pitting, and flash-memory wear are standard "phase-type absorbing Markov chain" problems: a component passes through ordered, irreversible damage states (micro-crack nucleation → propagation → critical length) driven by cycle count or charge/discharge count, with the final state being a discrete phase change (fracture, cell retirement) rather than "somewhat more worn." The contrast in piece (2) is real too: many systems specify a *global* end-of-life threshold (e.g., an SSD firmware that flips the whole drive to read-only once total cumulative writes cross a limit) that ignores which individual cells are more or less worn — a synchronous population rule layered over an otherwise per-cell sequential process.

**How to check it:** Fit failure-time data to a phase-type/Weibull hazard model and see if the shape parameter is consistent with a k-stage sequential process (increasing hazard rate consistent with needing k events) versus a single-threshold model. For the SSD case, this is directly checkable against SMART logs: compare per-block wear distributions at the moment of the global read-only flip against what a sequential per-block model would predict.

## 3. The analogical-transport piece (flagged, not endorsed)

Piece (3) — reusing "how far along the ratchet is" as a proxy for progress in an unrelated psychological or relational process — is exactly the move behind things like epigenetic "clocks" being read as biological/emotional age, or staged relationship/grief models (Kübler-Ross, social penetration theory) being used to gauge how "far along" someone is emotionally. I'd flag this rather than endorse it: the prompt's own diagnosis is correct that these mappings usually ride on shared vocabulary ("stages," "irreversible") with no argument for why an index built for one process should track a different one. The honest test, if someone wanted to use it, is a straightforward external-validity check: does the ordered index predict an independent outcome (e.g., does DNAm epigenetic age predict mortality *after* controlling for chronological age)? Where that's been done (Horvath/Hannum clocks), the correlation is real but modest — supporting the instrument only partially, not the strong "index = the underlying process" claim. For most relational-stage-model uses, no comparable independent validation exists, and I wouldn't claim the analogy works.
