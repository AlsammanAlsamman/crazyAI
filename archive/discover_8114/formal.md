**Essential structure**

Strip away the pole, the river, the moth, the rituals, and what's left is a fairly clean pattern:

A **mediating object sits at a boundary between two poles/identities** (call them A and B). It does not choose between them. Instead, it runs a **classification-and-accumulation loop**:

1. Incoming instances are sorted into two kinds — those that *resolve* (land, complete, become fixed/countable) and those that *don't* (remain suspended, unresolved, only "potential").
2. Resolved instances, taken in pairs, generate a **derived quantity** — a distance, a medium, a "space between" — that didn't exist before any resolved. (Two arrivals → one river.)
3. That derived medium is where the object's *state itself* becomes ambiguous — it sits in the space between A and B rather than at either pole.
4. Unresolved instances accumulate separately, and when they build up sufficiently, they **force continued operation of the mediator regardless of the resolution count** — the process runs even without the fuel that would normally justify running it.
5. The **terminal state is explicitly withheld**: the object's entire function is defined as *not reaching* A or B, but sustaining motion/decision between them indefinitely. Identity is redefined from "which pole" to "the ongoing act of not yet choosing."

So the invariant is: **classify → accumulate → let accumulation of the unresolved class override the resolved class as the driver of continued action → never let the driver terminate in either pole.**

**What kind of structure this is**

It's closest to a *non-terminating decision process* — something like a coin that is defined not by heads or tails but by the fact that it is always mid-flip, where "mid-flip" is treated as a legitimate, load-bearing state rather than a transient one. It has the shape of:

- a **two-valued classifier feeding a counter** (discrete, combinatorial), coupled to
- a **continuum interpolation between two end states** (topological/metric — "distance," "space between"), coupled to
- a **feedback rule that treats non-convergence as the success condition**, not a failure or a pending case.

That last part is what resists clean formalization. Most formal structures (fixed points, limits, decision procedures) are built to converge or halt; this one inverts the usual telos — it is a mechanism *engineered for perpetual non-convergence*, where the accumulation of "not yet resolved" instances is itself the thing that keeps the system alive. I can gesture at it as "a Zeno-process where the counter of unfinished approaches is what powers the next step," but I don't think it reduces to one standard formal object (not quite a fixed-point iteration, not quite a random walk, not quite a superposition) — it borrows the shape of each without being fully any of them. That resistance seems worth reporting as-is rather than forcing it into a single equation.
