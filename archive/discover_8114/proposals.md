## Two candidates that hold up, one that doesn't

**1. TCP congestion control (AIMD "sawtooth") — a control loop deliberately engineered never to converge**

How it maps: the congestion window sits at the boundary between "too small" (underutilizing the link) and "too large" (overflowing a buffer). Incoming packets classify cleanly into *resolved* (ACKed — landed, countable) and *unresolved* (in flight, or lost and awaiting retransmission). Successive ACKs are used pairwise (via RTT) to estimate a derived quantity — the bandwidth-delay product, literally "how much space/time separates sender and receiver" — and the window's correctness is only ever knowable in that derived space, not at either pole. Accumulating unresolved evidence (three duplicate ACKs, or a timeout) forces a multiplicative window cut *regardless* of how many packets were successfully delivered — the unresolved class overrides the resolved class as the driver, exactly as in the structure. And critically, RFC 5681's design intent is explicit: the window is never supposed to settle at a fixed rate; "additive increase" perpetually re-probes for more bandwidth, so oscillation *is* the correct, intended behavior, not a flaw en route to a fixed point.

How to check it: pull real cwnd traces (tcpdump + a cwnd-logging kernel patch, or ns-3/mininet simulation) over a long-lived flow on a stable-bandwidth link, and confirm the window never settles to a constant value as long as the path persists — it should keep sawtoothing indefinitely, with cut events time-correlated to duplicate-ACK/loss bursts rather than to any threshold on total bytes ACKed. If long flows on stable links do eventually flatline to a fixed window, the analogy fails.

**2. Hybrid zones in evolutionary biology (tension-zone theory, Barton & Hewitt 1985) — a boundary sustained indefinitely by an accumulation imbalance**

How it maps: two parental forms/species are the poles; the hybrid zone is the mediator sitting between them and never collapsing into either. Individuals born in the zone split into those *selected against* (resolved — fixed, removed from the gene pool, a countable outcome) and those still dispersing and mating (unresolved — ongoing potential, not yet acted on by selection). The zone's cline width — the spatial "distance" over which allele frequency shifts from one parental value to the other — is a derived quantity that exists only because of the balance between these two classes (width ≈ dispersal/√selection in the standard diffusion-selection model), and it is precisely in that width that the population's identity is ambiguous (neither pure species). As long as dispersal (the unresolved-accumulation term) stays above a threshold relative to selection, the zone is maintained rather than collapsing to fixation in either species — the unresolved class overriding the resolved class as what keeps the boundary "alive." The model's entire point is a *stable, non-zero* width — sustained non-convergence is the predicted equilibrium, not a transient.

How to check it: take a well-studied hybrid zone with independent estimates of dispersal distance and selection coefficient against hybrids (e.g., the European house mouse *Mus musculus/domesticus* zone, or *Bombina* toads), and test whether measured cline width tracks the tension-zone prediction and stays roughly stationary across generations/decades rather than narrowing toward zero. A steadily shrinking cline (eventual fixation) would falsify the "engineered for perpetual non-convergence" reading.

**A third I checked and dropped:** GNSS RTK carrier-phase ambiguity resolution looked promising at first (float solutions genuinely sit in a continuum between discrete integer-ambiguity states while epochs accumulate), but it fails the key criterion — the terminal state is *not* withheld by design; the whole point of RTK processing is to eventually fix the integer and exit the float state. That's a system trying to reach a pole, not one engineered to avoid it, so I'm not including it — forcing it in would be dishonest to the "never terminates by design" part of the structure.
