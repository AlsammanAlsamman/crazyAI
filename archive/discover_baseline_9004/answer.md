# STRUCTURE

**The Holonomy Ledger**

Take a finite set of operations O, together with a symmetric independence relation I ⊂ O×O (pairs that commute) and its complement D = O×O \ I (pairs that don't). This is the skeleton of a trace monoid: words in O* modulo free swapping of adjacent independent letters, which normally just tracks *which* orderings are equivalent, not *what it costs* to force an equivalence between orderings that aren't.

Add a group G — possibly non-abelian — and a debt map δ: D → G satisfying δ(a,b) = δ(b,a)⁻¹. A *ledger walk* is a sequence of adjacent transpositions turning one word w into a permutation w′ of it. Swapping an independent pair is free. Swapping a dependent pair ab → ba costs δ(a,b), and the walk's total debt is the ordered product of every δ incurred, read off in the order the swaps happened — order matters here because G need not commute.

The interesting object isn't a single ledger walk but the *set* of all ledger walks connecting the same w to the same target permutation w′. Different walks can, in principle, accrue different total debt. Call the structure **flat** at (w,w′) if every such walk yields the same debt (or the same conjugacy class, for a weaker version). Flatness is a local-to-global condition: it holds automatically for any pair of dependent operations swapped via a third independent one in two different orders iff a pairwise consistency condition on δ over triples holds — a discrete analogue of a curvature-free connection, where "curvature" is the commutator of debts around a minimal cycle of swaps (a 3-operation braid move, in effect).

So the structure has two regimes: a *flat* phase, where debt is path-independent and behaves like a genuine cost function on permutations (you can define distance-to-commuting as ‖ledger‖ unambiguously), and a *curved* phase, where the very act of reordering is history-dependent, and the "cost of reordering" question has no single answer — only a set of possible answers indexed by which swap-path you took, forming a torsor under the local curvature subgroup.

# PROPOSALS

**1. Trotter–Suzuki error bounds in Hamiltonian simulation.** Let O be the terms of a decomposed Hamiltonian (e.g. H = ΣHᵢ), let dependence be "Hᵢ, Hⱼ don't commute," and let G be the relevant Lie algebra/group with δ(Hᵢ,Hⱼ) = the commutator [Hᵢ,Hⱼ] (or its exponentiated group element). This is essentially the BCH-formula picture that already governs Trotter error: reordering the exponentials incurs error terms that are exactly commutators, and curvature in this ledger corresponds to nested-commutator (higher-order BCH) terms. This mapping is likely *recovering* known structure rather than adding new power — but it does suggest the flat/curved distinction as a practical diagnostic: **check** by computing the ledger's curvature (nested commutator norms) for a candidate Hamiltonian decomposition and comparing it against the empirically measured gap between different Trotter operator orderings in simulation; a numerically small curvature should predict low order-sensitivity.

**2. CRDT / operational-transform merge cost.** In collaborative editing systems, O = user operations, I = operations that provably commute (e.g. edits to disjoint regions), and δ(a,b) could be defined as the actual transform/merge cost (edit-distance, or size of the resolved diff) incurred when reconciling a and b in one order vs. the other. Flatness would mean "the total reconciliation cost of a batch of concurrent edits doesn't depend on merge order" — a genuinely testable property of a given OT/CRDT algorithm. **Check**: instrument an existing CRDT library, log operation pairs and their merge costs under different arrival/merge orders, and see whether total cost is order-invariant (flat) or varies (curved) — curvature would localize *which* triples of concurrent operations are responsible for order-sensitive merge blowup, which could directly inform scheduling/batching heuristics.

**3. Weak-memory-model reordering cost in compilers** — I considered this (debt = latency/hazard cost of reordering memory operations under an instruction scheduler) but I don't think it holds up as a distinct proposal: real memory-model cost isn't well-modeled as a *group-valued, invertible* ledger (costs like cache misses or stalls aren't naturally invertible or composable the way a group requires), so forcing it into this structure would be decorative rather than explanatory. I'll leave this one out rather than dress it up.
