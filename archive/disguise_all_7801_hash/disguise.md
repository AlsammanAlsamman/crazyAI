TRANSFORMED PROBLEM

Old Mabel runs a candle-dipping stall at the fair. She gets a little cloth sack of charms from each customer — buttons, shells, coins, feathers, whatever they toss in, anywhere from a handful to a hundred. Her job is to melt them all into one single blob of wax that captures the *whole* sack in its shape — and here's the trick her granny taught her: if a customer swaps out even one tiny charm for a different one, the final wax blob has to come out looking dramatically different, not just slightly different. A blob that barely changes when you swap one button is considered a bad blob — customers won't trust it as "theirs."

Mabel's usual method: she has exactly one blob of warm wax on her stick. She reaches into the sack, pulls out the next charm in whatever order it happens to be sitting, presses it into the one blob, and gives the stick a hard twist-and-fold before she's allowed to reach for the next charm. Granny was strict about the order of operations: you press, you twist, *then* you reach for the next one — never the other way around, never two charms pressed in before a twist happens, never a peek ahead at charm number nine while you're still folding charm number four into the wax. One blob, one hand, one charm at a time, twist before the next reach. And if the blob still looks a bit predictable, Granny's answer is always "just twist it a few more times."

Mabel's stall is popular, though, and the fair is loud and fast — customers are impatient, and she's starting to wonder if there's a quicker way to get an honest, well-mixed blob without babysitting one lump of wax through every single charm in strict lockstep.

SOLUTION 1
Do it exactly Granny's way. Keep one blob of wax on one stick. Empty the sack in front of you and take the charms in whatever order they land. Pick up the first one, press it into the blob, twist-and-fold the stick firmly, set it down only when the twist is done — only then pick up the second charm. Repeat, one charm fully pressed-and-twisted before the next is ever touched, all the way to the bottom of the sack. If the resulting blob still looks too plain or too similar to yesterday's, give it a handful of extra twists at the end for good measure, on the theory that more twisting can only help.

SOLUTION 2
Don't wait on charms one at a time. Dump the whole sack out on the table at once and grab a handful of stubby sticks instead of one — call over a few of your stall-neighbors. Hand each neighbor two or three charms at the same time and let them press-and-twist their own little nub of wax independently, all at once, nobody waiting on anybody else's turn. Once every nub has been pressed and twisted on its own, gather all the little nubs back to your own stick and fold them together with a few final twists into one blob. The charms still all end up inside one blob in the end, but nobody had to finish twisting charm four before charm nine got picked up — plenty of charms got read and pressed at the very same moment.

SOLUTION 3
Separate looking at the charms from melting them in. First, walk the whole sack out into a row of little labeled saucers on the table — just sorting and counting what's there (how many shells, how many buttons, which one was where in the sack), no wax touched yet, no twisting at all. Only after you've seen every single charm in the sack do you pick up your one blob of wax and start folding charms in — but now you're free to do it in any order you like, several charms shoved in per twist if you want, heaviest or rarest charms first, whatever seems best, since you already know the whole picture. Reading the sack and twisting the wax are two completely separate errands now, done back to back instead of charm-by-charm in lockstep.
