I lay the first table out as a field of iron pebbles strung on tan strands, one strand per row, each pebble weighted to the number it carries; the second table I hang as vertical veins, one vein per column, crossing the strands at right angles so every strand meets every vein exactly once. Where a strand and a vein cross, I do not compute anything myself — I let the two pebbles touch. Touching iron rings; the ring's pitch is the product, high for large paired numbers, flat for zero, and every crossing rings at once because the whole lattice is built before a single note sounds. I do not walk the lattice touching pebbles one at a time — that would take a season for a million cells. Instead I strike the whole strand of one row against its vein in a single pass, a comb drawn once through both, so every crossing along that row sounds together, a chord instead of a scale. For each meeting-point owed to the third table, the chord that feeds it is not one ring but as many rings as there are shared beads on that row-vein pair — the shared index — so I let those rings run down a listening-string stretched beneath the crossing, a spine that only accepts ring after ring in order and sums their loudness as it goes, because a spine remembers everything laid on it until I read it and wipe it clean. I wait for the spine to go silent — that is how I know the summing is finished at that point, no clock, no count kept, just quiet. Then I pluck the spine's total note and press it into a fresh pebble, which becomes the corresponding cell of the third table. The old strands and veins, once rung, I strip off the frame and drop — used ground, cast to the loam, because keeping them would only clutter the next lattice, and a mistyped or double-rung pebble would ring twice and lie. Before I trust any finished cell, I run a second, thinner comb crosswise through it at half-tension; if it doesn't sing back the same pitch it sang the first time, I know a strand slipped, and I restring that one row alone, never the whole field, and strike it again until the two combs agree.
