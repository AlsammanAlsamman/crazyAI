I lay the first table down the western face of a long wall in my city, one row of numbers to a brick, and the second table down the eastern face of the same wall in the other city — same stones, same street, but the wall is trained to dent only its own city's watches, so each side sees only its own bricks and must unsee the other's, exactly as custom demands. To multiply, I don't cross the wall myself — I send the grows. These are the spike-things that push up out of the swerve like weapons when summoned; I plant one grow at every shared index, and because a grow's root belongs to neither city, it alone may draw sap from both faces of the wall at once without triggering the melting. Each grow drinks one number from my row and one from the paired column opposite, and its thorns count out the product in clicks I can count by ear. I stand still and wait — a grow takes exactly as long to fruit as its two numbers are large — and when it fruits I pick the fruit, which weighs the product, and drop it into a waiting basket set at the crossing-point of that row and that column. A basket fills from many grows sown along the same shared index; when the last grow for that basket has fruited, I weigh the whole basket at once — that weight is the summed cell — and record it on the third table, a fresh wall built in the empty lot between the cities where both may look without unseeing. Spent grows I burn; baskets I empty and reuse.
