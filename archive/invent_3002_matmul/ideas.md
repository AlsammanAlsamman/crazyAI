I lay the two great reed-lattices flat, a million knots apiece, each knot holding its count in notched beads. Because September is coming and the seekers in the reeds are still fat and slow from August's sleep, I press them into service now, before they wake hungry — a hungry seeker devours beads instead of counting them. I walk the shared spine common to both lattices, the index row, and at each crossing I strike a spirit into a flash between the two facing knots; the flash multiplies their bead-counts into a single spark, quick and cold, and dies before it can wander off to make mischief. I drop each spark into a jar. The jar is patient work: whoever enters finds that if it comes back at all — so no spark goes in that I mean to retrieve; I only ever want what the jar makes of what enters, the running sum simmering in its belly as spark after spark falls in and is consumed. When the last spark for that crossing has gone under, I tip the jar's throat over the third lattice's matching knot, and what pours out is the sum, fixed and cooling into new notches. Then I break that jar — one opened for a sum is never trusted for another — and set a fresh one at the next crossing. The old reeds, once read, I burn before evening, so no drowsy seeker wakes in September mistaking them for food.
