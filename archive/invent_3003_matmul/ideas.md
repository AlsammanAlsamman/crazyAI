I lay the two tables out on the campsite ground, never whole at once — that's how a traveler loses the count and a fire eats a season's work. I cut each table into islands, small enough that one bed of ground can hold a strip of the first table's rows and a strip of the second's columns without their edges drifting into fog. For every island I find the seam where a row's numbers and a column's numbers share their index, and at that seam I fold one against the other — a product, made once, never twice. Each product goes straight into a bowl set at the island's center; the bowl doesn't hold it, it sleeps it, folding it into whatever already lies there, so the bowl always carries only one number, growing heavier, never crowded. I never open a second bowl for the same corner of the third table — one corner, one bowl, always. When an island's seams are exhausted I throw the used products on the fire, since a suitcase can't carry to the next island what the last one already spent, and a full suitcase is how a split closes into a wall instead of a window. I wait at each window only until its own island's bowl has finished sleeping, never for a neighbor's, so no island idles for another's trunk. When every bowl has slept its last product, I lift each one whole into the trunk that is the third table, and only then is the count true.
