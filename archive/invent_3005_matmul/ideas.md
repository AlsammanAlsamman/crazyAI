I stand at the weir between two banks, one table of numbers cut as notches into the antlers of the does who arm along the near bank, the other scored as rings burned into the coats piled at the far bank. The shared index is the current itself — I do not choose it, I only watch it go by, the same toward for every notch and every ring. For each moment the river carries, I pull one antler-notch and one coat-ring to the weir together and set them alight where they meet; the fire that takes is the product, and its heat I do not measure by eye but by how much snow it clears on the third bank's slope, cell by waiting cell. What melts there pools into the basin marked for that cell of the new table, and I let it pool through every moment the river offers before I read the water's line, because a sum read too soon is a lie. The spent antlers and blackened coats I throw on the fossilizing pile downstream — grass hardening to stone — since a fire once used cannot be asked to burn twice honestly. A million cells means a million weirs lit in the same afternoon, but the bank is long and the does many, so I let them all burn at once and wait only for the last snow-line to stop moving before I call the third table done.
