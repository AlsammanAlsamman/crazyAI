I string a black hair for every row of the first table and cross it, at a right angle, with a hair for every column of the second table, so the mound becomes a grid of crossings — one crossing for each cell the answer will need. At each crossing I pinch the two hairs into a knot, and the tightness of that knot is the resistance I tie it loose or hard depending on what the shared-index numbers say, since voltage is pressure and current is flow, and a slack knot lets more current pass while a hard knot chokes it. Down the length of each row-hair I hold a pressure equal to the first table's cell; along each column-hair I let current answer to the second table's cell; where they cross, the current that actually gets through is their product, no more, no less. All the currents crossing into one column-hair drain into a still pool at its foot, one pool per hair, and a pool does not move until every crossing has fed it — I wait, watching the stitched pillows on their stalks nearby stand taller the more a pool holds, since the pillows are gauges. When a pool stops rising I read its level as the summed cell, write it down, and cut that hair loose from the mound, throwing the spent length away so the tangle never outgrows what's still unread. I keep the wall and its puppeteers for checking only: shadows cast twice from two angles onto blank stone must agree in outline, or I retie a knot and drain that pool again.
