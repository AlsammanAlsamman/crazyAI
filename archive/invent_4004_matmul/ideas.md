I lay the two great tables out flat, along lines of dried bone circles that stretch from no beginning toward no landmark, because in this place a line is only ever visible from the harvest that made it, and a row unrolled here does not curl back on itself. Where a row from the first table crosses a column from the second, I set down a chest — the kind whose lid is shaped like a clock, though it tells no hour, only count. I wind it once for the shared index, and each tick turns up one bone circle from the row and one from the column, laid face to face. That facing is the argument: I do not calculate it in my head, I speak it, because only a spoken claim can be leapt by candle. For every argument spoken I light one sliver of a fresh candle and let it burn all the way down over that pairing — never blown out, never rushed — because a half-burnt candle is a half-made claim, and I will not forward what isn't finished. The wax drips into a bowl set under the chest, and when the clock's hand comes full round through every tick of the shared index, I weigh what's pooled there: that weight is the sum, the one true product of all the arguments made at that crossing. I write it once, on a fresh bone circle, and hand it to a runner, because nothing in this dimension may be carried backward — only forwarded, crossing to the island called Conclusions, which exists solely as the place things arrive at, never a place you set out from. I do not chase the runner; I wait, still, for the empty-handed return that tells me the circle landed. Then I break the spent chest, sweep the burnt stubs and the drained bowl into the flat dust, and move to the next crossing — and because this ground casts no shadow, a thousand chests can tick at once, side by side, none darkening another's candle, none needing a landmark to know its place, only its row and its column. A million crossings means a million small fires guttering out in their own time across the flat, a million bowls weighed, a million bone circles handed to runners who never look back — and when the last one lands on Conclusions, the third table is not built, it is simply arrived at, whole, from everything that was faithfully burned and forwarded and thrown away.
