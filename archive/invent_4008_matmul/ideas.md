I lay the first great table out along the vineyard rows, each cell an atom pinned to its own stake, electrons still circling it like patient moons, and I lay the second table across the kitchen floor as a mirror row of atoms, tiles black beneath the triangle-shaped pot the woman keeps by the window. To marry one table to the other I do not touch atom to atom directly — that is thief's work, and thieves have already stolen enough orbiting from this country. Instead I send the goose walking the shared season between them, the index-season common to both tables, and wherever her white foot lands on a numbered stake in the first row I make the cat with the red fur pounce the matching stake in the second column; each pounce plucks one electron from each atom and drops the pair, hissing with magnetism, into the pot. The fire before the kitchen takes each dropped pair and recites it the way it recites the father's weapon and the letters scale — number over number, a small multiplying chant — and what the fire finishes saying, I drop as a coin into the box that is money saved in this house. I wait until the goose has walked the whole shared season for that one stake-pair, the box filling coin on coin, nothing rushed, nothing skipped, because a season half-walked is a table half-true. When the goose stops, I pour the box — sum of all those coins — into the one new tile on the third table's floor, and I throw the empty box, the spent chant-ash, and the plucked electrons' husks onto the compost by the vineyard fence, keeping nothing that has already paid its due. Stake by stake, pounce by pounce, pot by pot, a million tiles get laid this way, the cat tireless, the fire never repeating itself twice for the same pair, until the third table stands whole, tile to tile, each number owing nothing more to be checked.
