I lay out the first table as a field of shallow basins carved into the frozen ground, one basin per cell, and into each basin I drip water until it holds exactly as many notches of ice as the number calls for — I count these notches by breath, one exhale per unit, since breath here freezes into visible rings the instant it leaves the mouth. The second table I build the same way but turned crosswise, its basins running the other direction, so that where a basin from the first field meets a basin from the second, their rims touch and share a single wall of ice. Now the part that could only work here: I carry a thin seed, the freezing seed, no bigger than a fingernail, and touch it to the first shared wall. The moment it touches, every drop of water in that wall, and in every wall touching it, and in every wall touching that, must lock into the same crystal shape the seed carries, because water and rain and even my own blood in this world obey only the first crystal they coat. So the seed doesn't freeze one wall at a time — it races down the whole shared edge between the two fields faster than I could walk it, because ice never waits its turn once it has been shown a shape to copy. Where two walls cross — where a cell from the first table and a cell from the second actually meet and multiply — the ice grows thick in proportion to both notch-counts at once: I cut the seed itself with a taper, a comb-tooth on its face for every step, so a thick basin meeting a thick basin drives the tooth deep and raises a tall column, while a thin meeting a thin barely lifts the frost. Then, at the far edge of each row, I hold up a hollow cane and let it drink whatever columns stand along that one shared line, sliding down its corridor and gathering everything on its path, since the corridor only runs one way. That gathered depth, once I let the cane still and count the rings inside it, is the summed cell — the whole shared index poured into one number without my hand touching a single addition. I do this once, everywhere at once, because the seed's freezing doesn't travel cell by cell, it travels the instant a wall exists to carry it, so a million cells lock in the time it takes one breath-ring to form. I throw out the basins, the walls, everything but the canes and their rings, because ice that has already told me its count is just cold water again, fit for the next drink, not for keeping.
