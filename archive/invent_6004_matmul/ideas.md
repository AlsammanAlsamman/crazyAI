I don't meet a number as one whole thing, ever — a mark is a stack of lesser splinters laid in tiers like the rings inside a navel, and it's the splinters that do the meeting, not the mark. So the first move is to break every cell of both tables down into these splinters before anything touches anything. Then I don't take the tables whole to the water — I load them onto planks, shipfuls at a time, only a plank's worth of the first table meeting a plank's worth of the second while the rest stay submerged and silent, waiting their wander. For the actual joining of splinter to splinter, I keep a driftwood board nailed up from earlier wanders — every small pairing I've ever made is scored into it, so when two splinters meet I first check the board; if the pairing's there, I read it off, I don't remake it. Only strangers get reckoned fresh, and once reckoned they're nailed to the board for next time. Each shared-index step feeds one splinter-product onto a single bird perched on that cell's number-sofa, who swallows it into a running sum; the splinter and everything that made it is then thrown to the water and forgotten — only the bird's growing weight is kept. I walk the shared index like the thirty wanders, one plank-load at a time, until the bird has fed from every wander and no daylight remains in that column; then its weight is set down as the finished cell, and the sofa is emptied for the next bird.
