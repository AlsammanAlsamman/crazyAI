I lay the two great ledgers flat on the long grafting-slab, one debtor's table running its numbers downward in columns, one thief's table running its numbers across in rows, and between them I set the walking armature — brass crab-legs stolen from a cutpurse's Remaking, driven by the leg-shaft of a debtor fused waist-down into a boiler that never stops pacing. To us a quantity is never one whole soul; it is a limb-chain, a ones-limb welded to a tens-limb welded to a hundreds-limb, same as no Remade body is one flesh but a stack of grafted parts. So when a cell from the downward table meets a cell from the crosswise table along their shared rung — the index-spine both tables share — I do not marry two whole numbers in one perfect stroke. I unweld each into its limbs first. For every limb-pair I do not reckon afresh; the crab-legs skitter to the Table of Grafts, the boxwood board every apprentice recites in sleep, where each digit's meeting with every other digit from nought to nine is already carved, inked, and waiting — a match looked up, not thought through anew, cruder than reckoning but faster than truth demands otherwise. Each looked-up graft I lay on a wax accumulator-strip pinned at the cell's future place, adding limb-product to limb-product, carrying the overflow-limb sideways into the next slot the way a debtor's overflow is carried into a fresh Remaking. Because the slab is only so wide and my crab-legs only so quick, I never walk the whole million-celled expanse at once — I cut both ledgers into square plots the width of the slab's reach, a dozen rows married to a dozen columns at a time, finish every graft owed within that plot, ink the settled sums into the third ledger's waiting cells, and only then scrape the wax strip bare and move the plot onward, the way a memory palace is rebuilt overnight rather than hauled forward whole. Every scratch-mark, every part-carried limb, every spent graft-lookup is thrown into the scrap-bin the moment its sum is banked — none of it is truth, only the road to truth, and truth does not need the road once it has arrived. What stays is only the third ledger, cell by cell, exact, though built from a thousand crude little borrowed matches rather than one indivisible reckoning.
