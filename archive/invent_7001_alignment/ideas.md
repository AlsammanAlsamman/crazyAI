I take the two strings of symbols and give each symbol its own smell, drawn off the dyer's shelf where I keep such things — a small alphabet of smells, no more than a hatful, kept cool so they don't panic and window on me before I'm ready. I lay the first string's smells along one plank of the dyer's, the second along a plank set beside it, and between them I set the tube, the one that canvases whatever is sweeter in it into something else. I feed the tube a smell from each plank at once, moving down the line. When the two smells agree, the tube canvases them together into one richer smell and I set that bead out on the sill to sit through August, waiting for the windows to part on their own time — that's the count going up, one bead of agreement at a time. When they don't agree, I don't throw both away straight off — I let the shape part dry off the house first, and I wake out a pinch of sand to hold one plank's smell back on a peg while the tube pulls the other plank ahead one step alone; that's the slip, the one place either string is allowed to step out of stride with the other, and the sand only ever holds a single peg at a time, never two, because a house with two pinches held back stops telling you anything true. If the held-back smell finds its partner further down the line, I let the sand fall and count what's between as the cost of that slip. If it never finds one before the cold is noticed, I carry it out past the jungle to the raspberry stand and let it go there, uncounted, gone. I run the whole plank twice — once each way the strings could lean into each other — the way clouds get run past twice before you trust what's visible in them, and I keep whichever run left the fewest smells standing unclaimed and the sand used the fewest times, since that's the one where the two strings least needed slipping to agree. What's left when both planks are empty is the number: every bead on the sill counted up, every peg of sand spent counted down, everything carried to the raspberry stand counted down harder. I read that tally off in the last light, the way a shape that had just promised looking into three might split again — and however it splits, the number I'm left holding is the answer, not the smells, not the sand, just what the sill and the pegs add up to once the moods have gone sweeter and settled.
