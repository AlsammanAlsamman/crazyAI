I lay the two strings of clay tesserae along the twin grooves of the stone calendar wheel, the outer ring and the inner ring, each beaded with the small alphabet's marks — never more symbols than fingers on two hands. I do not spin the whole wheel at once; that would be cheating time itself, and the ring only ends by returning to its own beginning. Instead I set the watchdog with the clock for a body at the center hub, and it ticks one notch at a time, and at each tick I read straight across, spoke to spoke, and where the mark on the outer tooth matches the mark on the inner tooth I lay down one bone-colored stone from the store-room shelves. Where they disagree, I lay nothing, and the gap stays bare clay. I do this once straight through, no slip, and I write the stone-count at the bottom of the shelf on a shard.

Then, because a string may have swallowed one extra bead or spat one out partway along, I let the watchdog choose a single weak point to strike — one seam, and one seam only, the way the boiler is allowed exactly one valve before it must overheat. At that seam I push the inner ring forward by one tooth for everything past it, so the two rings now walk out of step from there to the end. I count stones again. I do this for every possible seam in turn, one after the other, never two seams in the same pass — the princesses Rhyme and Reason permit only one exile at a time, not a full war of skips — and each count goes up on its own shard on the shelf.

When every seam has had its turn, the owls carrying pens fly along the shelf and I let them peck the shard with the most stones on it; every lesser shard I throw into the boiler to burn, argument lost, its claim undefended. The surviving number — the stone-count from the best seam, or from no seam at all if straight-through wins — is what I carry to the tollbooth market and have weighed openly, the way words there are sold by weight, so no one downstream mistakes it for anything but what it is: how well the two strings agree, allowing the world exactly one place to slip.
