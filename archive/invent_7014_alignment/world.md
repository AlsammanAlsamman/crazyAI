Her tongue locks in her mouth, moments flow past, the past is downstream and cannot be swum back to, branches that split into dialects. Men in bowler hats and dark coats hang in the air in front of ordinary houses, then a fish's open mouth. Time is a river, A boy lives on an asteroid so small he can watch the sunset forty-four times a day by moving his chair, He weeds out baobab seedlings before they can split the planet. All facing forward, A loop of string strung taut between two pairs of open hands, a fine fire runs just beneath her skin. Neither falling nor rising, In that instant she feels closer to the grass underfoot, Some events are rapids, than to anything she recognizes as herself.
