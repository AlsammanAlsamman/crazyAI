I take the two strings of symbols and thread each through its own eyelet on the same hinge, so they hang side by side and can be walked forward one bead at a time without either drifting alone. I walk both threads together, bead against bead, and at each pairing I press a hot pin through if the two beads match, leaving an ash-mark on the canvas cloth stretched beneath; where they don't match, I press nothing and the cloth stays bare there. Because one string may slip out of step partway through, I keep one slack-spool wound onto the hinge that I am allowed to pay out exactly once, at exactly one point along the walk, letting one thread skip a single bead while the other holds still and waits. I try the whole walk again and again, spending the slack at a different point each time, one attempt per point along the row, and for each attempt I count the ash-marks burned into that attempt's cloth. I throw away every cloth but the one carrying the most ash, since that is the walk that let the two strings agree longest despite the slip. I wait until every possible point has been tried before choosing, because stopping early would mean guessing where the slip belongs instead of finding it. The kept cloth, folded once into a paper rose, is what I hand over — its count of ash-petals is the number that says how well the two strings agree.
