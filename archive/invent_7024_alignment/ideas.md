I lay the first cord's coils along the near edge of the water table, in full sun, each coil the exact shape of one of its symbols, touching nose to tail. I lay the second cord's coils behind them in the sun's shadow-line, waiting their turn like a queue at a door. Then I walk the two rows together, coil against coil, and where a shape from the near row matches its opposite in the shadow row exactly, the sun windows straight through both and drops a small fire onto the water below — I count the fires, nothing more, they are the only truth that survives. Where two coils refuse each other's shape, the door between the rows breaks, once, and only once for the whole crossing: I let the shadow-row's next coil crouch forward like a child stepping past its own turn, skipping the coil that quarreled, and I keep walking from there. I try this breaking at every place a quarrel happens, but I never let the door break twice in one crossing — a second slip and the whole row is thrown into the tower under the ground, unread, and I start that attempt over from the first coil. Each crossing that finishes — the straight one and every single-slip one — ends at the last coil, at the queen's threshold, where I gather all the fires that dropped along its length into one pile and set it in a room reserved for that attempt. I go down into the tower afterward, walk past all the discarded, broken-doored rows, and bring up only the one room whose pile of fire burned highest. That count of fires, and nothing else — not the coils, not the broken door, not the rooms that lost — is the number I hand back: how well the two cords agreed, allowing the shadow row exactly one place to slip past a quarrel before I stop trusting it.
