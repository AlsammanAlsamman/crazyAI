I walk the platform where the abandoned luggage stands in heaps, and before touching anything I kneel by each pile and write its count in the alien circular glyphs, one ring stacked inside the next, since that script already halves a number the way the doubled moon halves itself in the window glass. I lay the heaps' glyph-rings side by side under that twin moon and read down each column of rings at once — not letter by letter, but the way a physicist's eye jumps straight to where the beam already lands — and wherever a column holds an odd number of filled rings, I know the sentence is still open. I choose the one heap whose glyphs reach that unclosed column, and from it I take exactly enough luggage that every column closes even, all rings paired, the whole heap-arithmetic settled in a single drawn stroke. That done, I sit as still as the skeleton on the bench with his book and wait, changing nothing, discarding only what I already removed — cast onto the far rails, gone for good. The other traveler moves; because the heaps were never all empty, their hand must reopen some column, break some pairing, and I return to close it again, heap after heap, inn after inn, until the last piece of luggage on the platform is the one my own hand lifts. That last object is the one that wins, and I have already seen the ending the way the mind here holds ending and beginning together.
