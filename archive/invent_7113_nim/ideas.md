I do not watch the hunter's barrel or the ducks' turnings; I climb instead the beauty-shelves that stand behind every triangle, since each flock, seen edge-on, stacks itself naturally into rows that double: one duck's width, then two, then four, then eight, each row a shelf of beauty holding as many birds as the shelf below held twice. I read each flock onto its shelves the way the nightingale reads script trees, forward and backward in one glance, and I let forgetting do the sorting: any shelf lit by an even number of flocks I simply let go unremembered, since paired light cancels and needs no further sight. What remains lit and alone, on the highest such shelf, is the mark I trust. If nothing remains lit anywhere, I know already, without one shot ever traced, that the hunter below empties his hands last and loses the ground he stands on - the ducks that lived, lived by pairing, not by luck. If one shelf still burns, I go to whichever flock lit it, and there, hidden among wingbeats indistinguishable to the eye, sits the leader - not chosen by plumage but by arithmetic, the single bird whose presence on that shelf cannot be forgotten away. I do not chase it through the sky; I simply trim that flock, duck by duck, only until its own shelves go dark exactly where all the others still burn, and hand the shot back knowing the hunter cannot recover before the last duck falls to whoever moves first with shelves already balanced against him.
