I take the pile of marks in their given order and walk them one at a time past the fire, because only fire falling on the chained row of sailors casts a shadow steady enough to trust as a token. For each mark I add one more knot to the sailor-chain — never breaking an old knot, only ever lengthening the row — then I count that mark's own strokes as pebbles into a small cup, and the cup's fill decides how far I swing the brick-incense on its short chain across the mouth of the piazza. The smoke bends the fire's throw onto the far wall, and the shadow landing there is never just the new knot's shadow — it is the shadow of the whole chain hanging between fire and wall, every knot before it included, because the whole line hangs together or not at all. I wait, always, for the smoke to thin to one straight thread before I read the shape the shadow makes against the wall's six fixed cracks, the same six every time, standing in for the six places my token will ever hold. Only when the shadow has gone still — never while it trembles — do I press a stub of wet clay to the wall to take its print, then carry that stub to the mouth of the stream and let the water run over it until only the hardened ridges remain; those ridges are the new mark of the token. I throw away the smoke, the cup of counted pebbles, and the old clay print the moment the new one is taken, since keeping any of it would let a hooded reader work backward from print to pile. When the last mark of the whole pile has passed the fire, the token is whatever ridge-pattern sits in my palm — small enough for one fist, unrepeatable, because moving even one mark anywhere back down the chain throws every shadow after it into a different bend, and the final print comes out a stranger to its neighbor.
