I take the pile of marks in their given order and lead them, one by one, down past the knowing walls to where the chained sit sharpening the blank pillar. Nothing about the pillar is read whole — each mark is fed singly to the nearest prisoner, who does not look at it, only feels how it changes the angle of what he was already sharpening, and passes that angle, not the mark, to the next in the chain. The angle carries backward through the line, so a mark dropped at the very start still trembles in the hand of the last one sharpening. Below them, in the horizonless pit, the river that floods every solid argument rises on each pass and does not recede until every mark has gone through; I wait for that recession, never before — a token pulled while the water is still up is worthless, the shapes beneath it still swimming. Anchored in the riverbed, past where any traveler has ever fused its source, stand the tiny wire shapes against the sun. They do not move themselves; the flood moves them, bending each wire by exactly the angle the last chained prisoner ground into the pillar on that pass. When the water finally goes down and the shadows stop their chitter — the insect-noise that means the walls are still settling — I climb to the pit's rim and read the silhouette the wires throw against the sun above: which lean, which stand straight, which cross another. That silhouette, small and fixed, drawn only once the flood is gone and the wires have stopped shivering, is the token. I keep nothing but that shadow-shape. The marks themselves I let the prisoners forget as soon as they're sharpened past; the pillar keeps sharpening onward as if nothing had ever passed through it; the floodwater drains wherever floodwater drains, unrecorded. I never ask the river where its source lies, because one mark changed anywhere in the pile bends the chain of angles differently from that point on, which bends every wire after it differently, and the silhouette above the pit comes back a stranger's shape entirely.
