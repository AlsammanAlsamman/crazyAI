I take the pile of marks in their given order and walk them, one at a time, past the servant who trails me with the inflated house on a stick — she swats it against my mouth to hold silence until each mark has been laid on the flat stone at the riverbank, so the order itself never gets spoken aloud and lost. I gather the marks into small heaps of even count, the way a musk deer gathers ground beneath it in fixed strides, and for each heap I send it racing across the mountain path in the deer's own pattern: uphill twist, downhill fold, a doubling-back that eats its own trail so no heap keeps the shape it entered with. Nothing here stays still except the final stone at the shrine, where the running shape gets carried forward into the next heap's racing — this is the part I never let ossify, because a calculation grown too satisfied with itself is a pillar that will bring the roof down on the whole working. I search the result in squares, the way the drifting man searches squares in every direction for the divine, turning the shape ninety degrees and folding its corners into its own center, again and again, until even a single mark changed at the start has smeared itself across every square by the end — I test this by changing one mark and watching the whole grid rearrange itself, and if even a corner survives untouched I throw the whole method away and start over at the riverbank. When the last heap is folded in, the owl takes the pen and calls the final shape inside the dreamer inside — fixes it, small and unchanging, the size of any other token regardless of how many marks came before. I never keep the intermediate heaps; they are burned at the shrine the moment the next heap swallows them, because a token that can be walked backward to its marks is no token at all — like the man who cannot recall being a man once he is entirely a butterfly, drunkenly satisfied among the flowers.
