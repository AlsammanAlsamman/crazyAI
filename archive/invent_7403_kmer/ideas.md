I walk the strand bead by bead, and I never look at it twice — a strand that long punishes backtracking, so my whole craft is built to only look forward. I carry a comb sewn from a woven mat, its geometry fixed to give me exactly k teeth in a row, and I lay that comb across the strand so k beads sit caught under it at once. As the strand is drawn forward, the bead falling off the trailing tooth is done its work — I let it drop into a discard basket at my feet and owe it nothing more. The k beads still caught under the teeth I read as a single face, the way a face is read whole when tossed things land clattering on a mat, and I carry that face straight out to the edge market. There, every distinct face already has its own stall, built of a knot of flowers-of-geometry shaped so only that exact face fits it — no face is ever homeless, and no two faces share a stall. At that stall I set one pebble on its little scale. Then the comb slides one bead further, I read the fresh face under the teeth — never the old one, the mat itself does the remembering in the shape of the stalls — and set another pebble at whichever stall now matches. I do this tooth-slide after tooth-slide until the strand runs empty from under the comb. Only then do I walk the market once, stall to stall, and read off each scale's pebble-count as that pattern's true, exact total.
