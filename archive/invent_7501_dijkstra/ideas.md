I set a pawn on the traveler's own square and give it an empty pouch — no eggshells in it, for home costs nothing to leave. Every road out from a square is a raspberry cane, and I already know its length because I broke it once into eggshell pieces, each piece the same weight in my palm; a long cane holds many pieces, a short one few. I loose the jungle's living snake down every cane leading from the pawn's square, and at the far end it drops into that place's pouch as many eggshells as the cane held plus whatever the pawn's pouch already carried — but only if that sum is smaller than what's already sitting there; a heavier delivery I throw straight back into the raspberry bushes and forget. When every reachable pouch has had its chance, my thumb presses a boulder onto whichever unboulered square holds the fewest eggshells of all — that count is fixed now, true forever, and the snake never visits it again. I copy that square's pouch onto a strip of board's paper, then send the pawn to sit at the next-lightest pouch and loose the snake again, cane by cane, boulder by boulder, until every reachable square wears both a boulder and a number. The squares no cane ever truly reaches — roads that dead-end loose in the jungle, leading nowhere useful — keep their pouches empty forever, and I leave them off the paper entirely.
