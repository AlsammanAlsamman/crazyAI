I fetch the board with two long rows of shallow pits — everyone in the market knows it, the counting game's board — and carve one more pit into its end grain for every place the traveler might need, joining pits with thread wherever a real road runs between them, the thread's length in fingerwidths standing for the road's own length. The traveler's own place gets an empty pit; every other pit I heap full to the brim with seeds, so full a jostle would spill them, because a place not yet reached must look like an abundance it hasn't earned. Then, each round, I choose whichever unsettled pit holds the fewest seeds and touch it with the blue flame that burns off someone mid-turning-into-a-bird — it doesn't scorch the wood, only marks that pit settled so I never count it again. Along every thread leaving that pit I walk a palmful of seeds at the unhurried rhythm of sowing, dropping one seed per fingerwidth of thread, and wherever that dropped count would sit lower than what's already heaped at the thread's far pit, I scoop that pit empty and pour the smaller pile in, remembering which pit sent it. Threads that never once lower a neighbor's pile — the ghost roads, the two ships riding at anchor under one claimed name, the corridor swept for no reason anyone living remembers — I cut loose and let the ivy take, the way sailors let a rotten plank go overboard rather than carry it into the next port. When every pit wears the blue mark, I read the board straight across: each pit's final seed-count is the shortest road there is, and by following backward along whichever thread last lowered each pit, I walk the traveler's own path pit by pit, back to the empty one where they began.
