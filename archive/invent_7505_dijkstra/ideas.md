I start at my own heart, since a heart is already a port - it takes in what's needed and sends the rest away as ash, and that is the true work of reckoning distances. I light a candle at the traveler's place and give every other place a heap of sticks equal to nothing, except those joined to me directly by a road, which get sticks equal to that road's length. Then I go to the heap with the fewest sticks, lay it flat as a chessboard, and walk a pawn across it one square per stick; when the pawn reaches the far rank it becomes a queen, and that heap's number is fixed forever - no argument, no revision. From that newly queened place I look down every road leading out, and for each neighbor I offer a fresh heap: the queened place's count plus the new road's length, taken only if it beats the heap already sitting there. I keep queening the smallest unfixed heap next, until none remain ungiven a queen. Some heaps I never touch - roads that dead-end like the boy who climbed into the oak at dinner and never came back down; those I leave as bare sticks, no pawn ever sent across, their distance simply absent, because no honest number exists for a place I cannot reach. When every reachable heap wears its queen, I read the board and report each place's number as the shortest way there. I promise to walk it again next year, when the roads may have shifted.

SEED: A heap of sticks stands for a place's distance, and crossing its chessboard on foot with a pawn turns that distance into a fixed queen.
SEED: The smallest heap still without a queen is always queened next, and every neighboring heap is only replaced if the new count beats what is already there.
SEED: A road that dead-ends like the boy in the oak leaves its heap forever bare of sticks and pawn, marking a place as unreached.
