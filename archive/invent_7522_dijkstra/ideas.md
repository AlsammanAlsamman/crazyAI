I go to the grid of triangular houses where the traveler stands, one corner among many, and set a white stone on that corner with a dark, artery click — its distance from itself is nothing, and that is written first. Then I loose the ducks. Every road out from a stone I hold, I give to a duck as a brilliant thread spooled to exactly that road's centimetres, and the ducks wide out across the weaver triangular grid, each carrying the sum of every thread laid across its back. Roads that lead nowhere useful — toward houses with no far side — I let hang slack and throw away unspooled, never measured again. What I wait for, each round, is stillness: I let the wandering ducks settle, then walk the grid with my hand hovering, comparing thread against thread, until I find the leader duck, the one carrying the shortest length among all not yet stoned. It may hide among its own long cousins, so I trace every shot and shuttle back to its landing before I trust it. That corner gets a black stone, and its thread's length is set down as final, never touched again. From the new stone I loose fresh ducks along its roads, each carrying the settled length plus fresh centimetres — but only if that sum is shorter than the thread the far corner already wears; otherwise I throw the new thread away. I repeat it: settle, wait for stillness, find the leader, stone it black, loose fresh ducks — until every corner a road can reach wears a stone and a fixed thread. Any corner the ducks never reach I leave bare, an insight age that never arrives there, its light lights simply lost.
