I go first to the place where the traveler stands and set a single ember there, banked so low it is nearly out but never out, and I mark that spot with the round wooden disc etched with one character, laid behind no soldiers yet because nothing there needs guarding. Every other place in the land gets an unlit heap of sticks and only cold ash, since I do not yet know how far it is. Then I send runners down every road at once, each carrying that road's own length notched into a stick, and where a runner reaches a heap whose home already holds a lit ember, he lays a new stick on the far heap whose length is that ember's number plus his road's notch. Some roads run only to places already ringed by their own garrison, patient as stone, refusing entry - those sticks the runners drop by the wayside, and I feed every dropped stick straight into the nearest hearth, because a fire that isn't given fuel goes out, and this one still has streets to warm. When all runners have returned, I do what the fortune-tellers do at the pyramid: every heap throws its sticks into the air together, one throw for the whole land at once, and where they settle I read straight across a single row, one sliver from every heap in the same breath. Whichever sliver in a heap is shortest becomes that place's new ember, and I burn every longer sliver from that same heap for fuel, keeping only the winner as a coal banked forward to the next throw. A garrison rides in and seals any heap whose ember matched exactly what it held after the throw before - that place is finished, its ember will not shorten again no matter what still arrives, and its own runners stop going out for good. I keep throwing, sealing, burning, and sending until every heap wears its garrison, or until a heap's ash never once catches a spark across throw after throw - and that heap I mark as nowhere useful, a road leading only into a dead field the elephants never walked, its distance forever unlit. When the last garrison has ridden in, I have the hooded figure read the whole land's embers aloud in one pass, place by place, and that recitation is the answer: the shortest way from the traveler's disc to everywhere the light ever reached.
