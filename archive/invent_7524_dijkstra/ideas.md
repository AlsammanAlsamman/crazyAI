I hang one polished jewel for every place, threaded into the infinite net, and the traveler's road-knots are lengths of blood-dark cord, some short and taut, some long and slack, running jewel to jewel. I touch only the jewel where the traveler stands, and that touch becomes a glint that must travel every cord it can reach, moving no faster than the cord is long — a short cord flashes at once, a long one keeps the glint waiting on the way. I do not chase the glint; I crouch by the chalk grid scratched in the pavement, one square per jewel, and I wait, marking in white dust the instant each square's jewel first catches fire with light, because a jewel already reflects every other jewel touching it and needs only its first true flare. Whatever flare reaches a jewel afterward, arriving late along some longer, slacker cord, I throw away without marking twice — the first light is the only light that counts, and the rest is wasted color no dyer's cloth would keep. Any jewel whose square stays dark, that no glint ever reaches, however long I crouch, I leave blank and cross with a scratch, the way the desert crosses out its own tracks: that place is a road leading nowhere useful, and I will not send the traveler chasing wind. When every reachable square carries its one chalked number, I read the grid like a hopscotch child reads her own thrown stone, and that is the whole of every shortest way, from the one touched jewel to all the rest.
