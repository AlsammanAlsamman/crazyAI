I take a nightingale for every road out of the traveler's place and loose them all at once, and each bird sings one note for every stride of road beneath it, growing hoarser with the distance, until at the first cross-marked board along its path it sings itself to death — and the note it dies on I carve into the rigid lattice of old memory that keeps one fixed slot for every place in the land. Where two dying notes reach the same board, I keep only the fainter one, the lesser hoarseness, and let the louder rot in the desert with the roads that lead nowhere at all; those dead roads I mark once and never send a bird down again. But a death-note is not always the truest way in, so at that board I open a cow's head — inside it a woman milks a smaller cow, and what she draws out is the note as it would sound coming instead by way of the last board I settled, plus the stretch between; if her milk is thinner than what's already carved, I scrape the lattice clean at that slot and cut hers in its place. Board by board I go this way, always settling next whichever unsettled slot holds the quietest note, never touching a settled board twice, never sending a spent bird again. What's thrown away stays thrown away. When the last nightingale falls silent and no cow yields a thinner milk, the lattice is finished — every reachable place bears its permanent carving, every place past reach stands blank as the pink-brown desert with no landmark — and I read the traveler the whole lattice in order.
