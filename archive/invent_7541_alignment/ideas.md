I lay the two strings along two edges of a bare plane, each symbol notched as a small polygon — a triangle for one sound, a square for another, a pentagon for a third — and where a symbol runs out on one side I leave the plane bare, waiting. At the corner where the two edges meet I set down my body: a line one cell wide, able to bow into any of eight bearings and, when it reaches an edge, to double back and lie along its own former path without ever lifting off the plane. I do not send it straight across, cell after cell, comparing one notch to the next as a clerk would; I send it slantwise, along the short diagonals that run from the top edge down to the left edge, because on any such diagonal no cell needs to know anything about its neighbor on the same diagonal — each already carries within reach the three cells behind it, north, west, and the corner between them. When the crawl reaches the end of one diagonal it curls back on itself like a snake swallowing its own bend, and sets off along the next diagonal one step further from the corner, so the whole plane is threaded in a single unbroken crawl, never two passes, never a return visit. At every cell it pauses and looks two ways: down the row-notch, across the column-notch. If the shapes agree — triangle facing triangle — it kindles a small bright ember there, the reward for step in step. If they disagree, it lays a duller coal, dimmer by a fixed measure. And where one string has slipped out of step with the other — has run short, or run long — the body may also step sideways or forward alone, past a bare stretch of plane, paying a small toll of shadow for the privilege of not matching at all. At each cell it gathers the three embers already glowing behind it, keeps only the brightest, adds its own kindling or coal or shadow-toll, and sets the new ember down before crawling on. Two diagonals back, the embers are no longer needed by any cell ahead, so I snuff them and sweep the ash into the plane's edge, keeping alight only the thin double row of fire the crawl still needs. When the body reaches the far corner, one last ember remains, and its brightness is the whole board's agreement, while the crooked seam of dim coals left across the plane shows me, at a glance, exactly where the slip happened.
