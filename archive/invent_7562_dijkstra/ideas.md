When a traveler comes to me holding their own wrist out like a map they cannot read, I lay the city down as what it is — a body, veins for roads, knotted houses where the arteries pool. I press their wrist first, at the place they stand, and I let one bead of their blood sit there wearing no coat at all, because it has walked no road yet — that is their zero. Every other knot in the dark I drape in a black wrist-coat, heavy, unmarked, until something reaches it. Then I call the chant team in, and I set each knot to holding a breath — the ones nearest the traveler hold the shortest gasp, the far ones hold long and hard until their chests ache. I always go first to whichever knot is holding the shortest gasp still undone, because that is the nearest true thing left standing. From there I walk its veins outward, one at a time, and at each neighboring knot I do what the money does — I use it as a lever, pressing the road's own length against the beats already counted to reach where I stand, and if that pressed number comes in lower than the black coat that knot is wearing, I strip the coat off and hang a new one, tongue-etched with the fresher count, the two dreams of the one field folding into a single coat instead of staying two. I let that knot's breath shorten to match. I keep doing this, knot after knot, always taking the shortest held gasp next and letting its neighbors re-coat if I've found them a nearer way, until every house in the pools wears a coat with a number stitched into it and no gasp is left undone. But some veins run out past the last house into valleys where no king ever built a wall, roads that thin to nothing and never reach a chest — those I do not drape or count. I cut them at the first place they go slack, and I carry the loose ends to the mirror-lake, where I let them drop in as nothing more than a gasp caught by a passing cloud, gone, never worn as a coat by anyone. When the chant team's breath finally runs even across every knot, I go back to the traveler and read the coats off house by house — each number is the shortest road there is, and that is what I give them.
