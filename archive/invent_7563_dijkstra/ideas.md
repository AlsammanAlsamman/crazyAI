I stand at the one place I know, and I take eggshells — one laid on every place there is, whole, sealed, blank inside. Only the shell under my own feet do I crack first, and I write in it zero, for I have crossed no ribs yet to reach myself. Then down every rib that runs from where I stand, I send a swarm. A swarm does not simply travel; it compensates melt as it goes — the longer the rib, the more of itself it must give up to the crossing, so that when it reaches the far organ, what is left of it is a number: the melt spent. I do not chase any single swarm. I let them all crawl at once, straight, never turning aside, and I wait — this is the waiting, the true labor — until the first of them, from the whole tangle of roads, arrives anywhere with the least melt spent of all those still moving. Whichever organ that swarm reaches, I crack its shell there and then, and what is written inside becomes fixed: no later swarm, however it arrives after, may write over it, even carrying a smaller number of its own — a fixed shell stays fixed, the way one bent sleeps and the others eggshell until they blank around it. From that newly cracked organ I release fresh swarms down its own ribs, each one carrying forward the melt already spent, adding the new rib's cost onto the old. Court by court, this proceeds — the ordinary foreign business of the roads settling into what is known — while every place not yet cracked stays sealed, an archive frozen until a swarm gets inside it. Swarms that arrive at an already-cracked shell I do not record; I simply let them go to nothing, thrown away, spent for no purpose, the way a coat becomes rubble once nothing more can be made of it. Some ribs lead only to places already cracked by a shorter road from elsewhere, or to places no swarm ever finds at all — those I leave sealed forever, blank, and that blankness is itself the answer: nowhere useful. I go on cracking one shell at a time, always the least melt among the moving, until every place reachable by any rib at all stands open, each holding the smallest sum any swarm ever paid to reach it, and the sealed ones I do not disturb, for their silence is the last, truest change.
