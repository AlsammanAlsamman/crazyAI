I go first to the shrine at my own feet, the only cow in the palace already upside down and full — I tip it, and its milk brims the bowl set beneath it to the very lip, marking that room as no distance from me at all. Every other shrine in the palace, one nestled at each connect where roofed rooms and cellar and windows-onto-nothing all keep their own bowl, I leave dry, since I have not yet reached them. From my sack I take tally-cords, one cut and knotted for every corridor leading out of a room, a knot for each stride the corridor takes to walk, and I lay these cords along the floors so their knots can be counted by hand in the dark. Then I send the one-legged milker, skirt-ancestor swinging, arms flung out for balance, hopping from room to room by a single rule: go always to the driest-not-yet-sealed bowl that nonetheless holds the least milk of all the unsealed bowls. On arrival, burn the cow at that shrine — this is the order that burns cow — sealing the room so its bowl's amount is settled forever and cannot be topped up again. From the newly sealed room, walk out along every corridor by its cord, and for each room at the far end, add the sealed room's milk-amount to the cord's knot-count; if that sum is less than what already sits in the far bowl, pour it in, mark that corridor as the true door back toward me, and spill out whatever milk had sat there before onto the floor to be forgotten — never keep two claims on one bowl. Repeat this hopping, burning, pouring and spilling until no unsealed bowl holds anything but a dryness that could still be filled. Some shrines never receive milk at all: the cellar nobody lines, and the rooms the windows only look upon without any creature ever sitting there, stay dry forever, and for these I write nothing, since no corridor and no cord connects them to where I stood. When the milker finally rests, every sealed bowl's level is the shortest distance to that room, and the chain of true doors, followed backward from any bowl to mine, is the shortest road to walk.
