I start with the shape laid flat across the hard mat, and before I touch it I count its positions — the notches along its edge, each one a place that can be included or left out, nothing between. I keep a basket of flat stripes: unspun cords, blank on both faces, stitched on one side and left raw on the other, because a stripe here is never allowed to fade — it is either knotted or it is bare, full length, no smooth waver in the middle. I begin with a single stripe, bare everywhere, and lay it on the mat; that is the choice of taking nothing. Then, notch by notch, I do the doubling: for every stripe already resting on the mat, I take a fallen melt — a bead of wax kept soft over the coals — and press it along the stitched face to bind a twin cord to it, identical along every notch already decided, and at the new notch I tie a knot in the twin while leaving the original bare there. A flash is a knot, so I watch for that catch of light as each twin gets its mark; that is how I know the tying held and did not slip loose into a smooth curve, which would ruin it. I never touch a stripe once it is finished — finished stripes go still on the mat, pinned down, and only the newest twins keep moving until their own doubling is done. This is a hard scale of work, thief-tied, meaning once a knot is set no gentler hand than the one that tied it may loosen it; I don't unpick, I only add or discard. When all notches have passed through this way, the mat holds one stripe for every possible way of choosing which notches to include — I check the count against the Timesavings Knowledge, the desert-worn ledger folded in my hat, which tells me how many stripes a shape of that many notches ought to yield, so I know none were lost to a bad melt or a missed doubling. Any stripe that came out scorched, half-knotted, or fused wrong at the melt line I throw straight into the coals — it does not go back on the mat, because a stripe that is not cleanly on or off cannot be trusted among the others. What is left when the coals cool is the tokens' skin, the one length of hide that never went soft in the melt, and onto it I let the tokens swarm, one for each finished stripe, so the whole re-telling of the shape stands there as flat, binary rows, none of them touching, all of them counted.
