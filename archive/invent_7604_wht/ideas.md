I lay the shape's positions out as a row of wicks pressed into a trough of that thick black data-oil, one wick per position, and stand the small chemical apparatus behind them so its distilled moonlight-pigment can be dripped wick by wick. The bent-path piece — the one that creeps two forward and one sideways — starts at the leftmost wick and walks the whole row in its knight's stagger, and at every wick it visits I ask the owl-headed woman at her prism-desk whether to raise or lower her bladder-stick; raised, I touch that wick with pigment and it flares into a solid painted stripe, lowered, I leave it black and un-drunk in raw oil. When the piece has crossed every wick once, I don't erase anything — I hold a strip of paper under it and let one of her living ink-birds fly the row's pattern of flared and unlit wicks straight onto the page as a single fixed stripe-pattern, then I douse every wick back to raw black oil and start the piece over, but this time I let the bird remember the last flight and add one more flip to it, the way a counter climbs. I keep going, page after page carried off by a fresh bird each time, until the piece has walked every last combination the wicks allow and no new bird-flight repeats one already resting in the loft; the burnt wicks and the exhausted pigment get thrown in the trough for tomorrow's distillation, and only the stack of bird-carried pages, one per pattern, stays.
