I never keep the shape as it first floods over me. I lay the whole curve along a bone-colored plank and drive a nail at every place the tree of dried genders would stop and ask its round-side-up question — those nails are my positions, and they do not move again; they are the only still things in the whole working. Then I walk the token-rooms, and there is one room for every cluster of nails a hand could ever point to, from the empty cluster to the cluster that takes every nail at once. In each room I lay a fresh row of tokens along the plank, one token per nail: sun-side-up over a nail that belongs to that room's cluster, iron-blade-down and winter-cold over one that doesn't — never half-turned, never warming toward kemmer, always fully one face or the other. I wait for the flood driven up under the plank to settle flat before I read what the shape was doing at just the nails left sun-side-up in that room, and I score that single number onto the token-row before it dries. Once a room's row is scored I throw the room's flood-water out and never revisit it. When every room has been walked — every board, every split, every mid-division of the nails — I am left holding a stack of flat stripe-rows, one per room, each one either wholly lit or wholly shadowed at each nail, and that stack, not the original curve, is what I hand back as the shape re-told.
