I take the message dust-mote by dust-mote, each grain already a small jigsaw cell of its own, red on black, gathered from the grass where the sky's waterfalls pour endlessly through the disc's rim. I lay them in a row along the garden path, one cell to a stone. To build the seal, I walk the row a second time carrying a blank cell of the same red-on-black cloth, and at every stone I press the blank against the mote lying there — where both show red, the pressing blackens it out entirely, since that is the arithmetic this world keeps: a thing pressed onto itself is gone, not doubled. I do this twice more, once walking only the even stones, once only the odd, so I end with three trailing cells instead of one, each holding a different mixture of what came before. I wait until the rim-waterfalls below wash a fine new dust over the three cells, which fixes their red permanently the way frost fixes a footprint — until that settling happens the seal can still be pressed wrong and ruined, so I touch nothing. I throw away nothing from the row itself, but the order in which I walked it I let dissolve from memory, because the garden path itself remembers it, stone by stone, and that is the only ledger I trust. If later a stone's mote is scoured blank, I press the three sealed cells against whatever motes still hold their red, and the missing one blackens back into red where it belongs.
