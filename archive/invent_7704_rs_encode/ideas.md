I carry the message's word-tokens — flat coin-words bought at the tollbooth market — to the weighing stalls where the moonwright keeps her scale, because in this kingdom the continuous is built around the moon and every value must be read off her dial before it means anything. I lay the tokens in order along the beam, still, one at a time, and wait for the moon-dial's needle to settle each time before moving to the next; a token that hasn't stopped swinging hasn't confessed its true weight yet. Once every token has confessed, I carry their confessed weights up to the sun, whose sky is jigsaw cells, black-outlined, red-filled, each cell a place a weight can sit. I press the weights into the cells two and two: where two equal weights land on the same cell they erase each other clean, the way this world's arithmetic insists a thing added to itself vanishes; where they differ, the cell keeps whatever residue is left burning red. I keep folding the sky this way, halving the cells each fold, until only a short row of stubborn red cells remains — these are the seal-tokens, fewer than the message, holding in their leftover red exactly what combination of originals produced them. I take that short row and leap it, under gravity, out to the island called Conclusions — the leap's arc, long or short, fixes the order of the seal so it can't be scrambled later. Only then do I pour the whole run, message and seal together, through the cupbearer's cup, since hers is the only door out of the market and nothing leaves uncounted; what doesn't survive the pour — spent chalk-marks, cancelled duplicate weights — I throw down the tollbooth's coin-slot, gone for good. If later a few tokens come back scorched unreadable, I weigh what's left, refold the same sky, and where a cell now disagrees with its seal-twin, that disagreement is the erased original, restored.
