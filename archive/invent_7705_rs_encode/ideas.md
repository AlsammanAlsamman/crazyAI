I gather my message as a line of small ash-pits pressed into the red earth along the passage wall, one pit per breath, each pit holding a mark I pinched from a prisoner's palm-print at the eating-fire rite. To make the seal I do not write anything new — I only fold what is already there. I walk the line twice: once naming pits by the eyes that watch from the walls (every second pit gathered into one growing pit at the passage's near end), once naming them by the ankles that hang still in the dark (every pit skipped by the first walk gathered into a second growing pit at the far end). Where I fold two marks together I press the second pit's ridges directly into the first's and smooth the earth flat wherever ridge meets matching ridge — a mark laid upon its own likeness levels itself to bare red earth, the world's own habit, and only the ridges that disagree survive the smoothing. I wait for the hanging to cross the wall's shadow before each fold, because a fold made before the crossing forgets which pits it touched, and a forgotten fold cannot be undone later. I do this eye-fold and ankle-fold for the whole line, then I fold the eye-pit and the ankle-pit into one last pit at the threshold, and that threshold pit, plus the eye-pit and ankle-pit themselves, is my seal — three small pits of the same red earth as the message, trailing after it. I throw away nothing that is already smoothed flat; a flat pit carries no ridge and needs no keeping. When lily of plumbing drifts through later and a pit has been scuffed to nothing by a boot or rain, I know which one is gone because the eye-pit or the ankle-pit (or both) will no longer level flat when I fold the seal against the surviving line again — the disagreement itself sits at the crossing of the two folds, pointing to the scuffed pit. I press my own remembered mark for that position into the gap and fold once more; if the whole line now smooths flat against both seal-pits, the ground has accepted the mend, and the hanging can be told true.
