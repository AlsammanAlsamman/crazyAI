I walk the row of lanterns at dusk, when each one burns two flames from a single wick-fork: the bright flame straining upward and, cupped beneath it in a dish of black glass, its shadow flame, which can lean toward the wick (a taking) or away from it (a giving) — that lean is the sign the lantern carries. I do not choose which lanterns the rule visits; the rule comes on its own feet, walking the shadows cast by a handful of sand grains scattered at the foot of the row, and because no two grains agree on where the sun stands, no two shadows point the same way, and where a shadow's tip from one grain touches the base of another lantern, that lantern and the lantern it was cast from are named a pair for this visit. I crouch at each named pair and hold between them, at arm's length, one coin from the satchel I never let go of, turning it slowly until its two faces catch the bright flame of the first lantern and the shadow flame of the second at once. If the coin warms — if both faces glow the same color — I know the two flames are truly one creature seen from opposite sides, the way the coins in my satchel are drowning-things wearing gold only because I am looking at them from above; so I pinch the wicks together and let them burn as one flame, the sum standing where two stood. If the coin stays cold on one face while the other scorches my fingers, the flames were opposites only pretending to be two, like a duck that would rather vanish entirely than be traced back to its own flock, and I pinch both wicks out together, dish and all, and drop the blackened wick-fork into the tide pool at the root of the row, where the water keeps things it does not explain. I do not touch the lanterns the rule's shadows never named; they burn on exactly as they stood, waiting for a sand grain to fall differently another dusk. When the coin in my hand no longer warms on any face I try, I know the visiting is finished, and I let the tide take the last of the ash.

SEED: Sand-grain shadows disagreeing on the sun's position select which two lanterns the rule pairs for a visit.
SEED: A satchel coin held between a bright flame and a shadow flame is read for matched or opposite warmth to decide fusion or cancellation.
SEED: Cancelled wick-forks are pinched out together and dropped into the tide pool at the row's root, discarded for good.
