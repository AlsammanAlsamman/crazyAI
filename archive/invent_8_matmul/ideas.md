I take the first table and cut it into thin puppet-rods, one per row, each notch burned along the rod's length to a certain pitch-depth so the value holds still no matter how the fire gutters. The second table I wash onto sheets of stretched foam-skin, one column per sheet, translucent, each cell a layer of pigment thickness that lets more or less firelight pass. For every rod I hold it steady before the flame, and behind it, at the matching height, I hang the paired foam-sheet; the shadow that falls on the cave wall there is fattened or starved by the pigment it crossed, and that shadow's width is one term of the product, waiting. I never read a shadow just once — I let it fall onto wet clay laid the length of the wall, and where shadows from different rod-sheet pairings land on the same clay mark, sharing the same shared index, the clay darkens again, a layer atop a layer. The chained ones at the wall's foot are my count-keepers, one bound to each output mark, turning their heads only when a new shadow crosses their spot, knotting a cord at the wrist for every darkening they feel under a dragged thumb. When the last rod has passed the last sheet, I douse the fire, walk the wall reading knots into the third table cell by cell, then scrape the clay smooth and split the rods for kindling — they were scaffolding for a count, nothing more, and a wet world doesn't hoard what it has already used.
